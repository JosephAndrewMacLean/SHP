## Purpose

The primary goal of the Physician Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy is to ensure that patient
care remains of the highest quality when physicians are on leave. The organization is
committed to providing uninterrupted healthcare services, maintaining appropriate
clinic access, and preserving new-patient appointment availability.

This policy establishes a consistent process for planned and organized physician
absences while ensuring that sufficient physician-led clinic capacity remains
available within each service line.

## Policy

Given that multiple physicians may request time off simultaneously, physicians are
expected to work collaboratively with their colleagues and operational leadership to
ensure appropriate coverage of clinics and continuity of patient care.

Physician PTO requests may be denied when:

- Approval would result in inadequate physician clinic coverage within the applicable
  service line;
- More than one physician within a service line is requesting overlapping PTO and
  required clinic availability cannot be maintained;
- The requesting physician does not have sufficient available PTO to cover the
  requested absence; or
- The requested absence would otherwise materially impact patient access, clinical
  operations, ASC operations, or other business requirements.

Physician clinics must be maintained by physicians. Physician Assistants (PAs) may
assist with a physician's clinic; however, a PA may not independently replace the
physician clinic capacity required under this policy. A physician must be present and
actively conducting the clinic for that clinic time to count toward the required
physician clinic availability.

The organization will seek to maintain at least **66% of the total expected physician
clinic capacity** for each service line during periods of physician PTO. The purpose
of this threshold is to protect patient access, particularly availability for
new-patient appointments.

When multiple physicians are scheduled to be off at the same time, the remaining
physicians within the service line may be required to provide additional clinic
coverage in order to maintain the minimum service-line requirement. If sufficient
coverage cannot be established, one or more PTO requests may be denied.

## PTO Request Requirements

1. **Extended PTO Requests:** Physicians requesting PTO for an extended period,
   defined as five (5) or more consecutive workdays, must submit the request no less
   than three (3) months prior to the first requested day of PTO.
2. **Submission Method:** Physicians are required to submit all PTO requests in
   writing to `[EMAIL ADDRESS]`. A PTO request is not considered formally submitted
   until receipt has been confirmed by the Scheduling Team.
3. **Operational Review:** All PTO requests, regardless of the length of the requested
   absence, will be evaluated for their impact on patient care and continuity,
   new-patient appointment availability, physician clinic coverage, clinical
   operations, ASC operations and surgical coverage when applicable, and other
   relevant business or operational requirements.
4. **Approval Communication:** All approvals and denials of physician PTO requests
   will be communicated by the Director of Clinical Scheduling to the requesting
   physician and applicable clinical and ASC teams.

## Minimum Physician Clinic Coverage by Service Line

The following clinic-capacity standards represent the minimum physician-led clinic
availability that must generally be maintained during physician PTO.

| Service Line | Full-Time Physicians | Standard Clinic Capacity | Minimum Clinic Days | PTO Coverage Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orthopedics – Service Line A | 6 | 18 | 12 | Maintain at least 12 physician-led clinic days/week |
| Orthopedics – Service Line B | 4 | 12 | 8 | Maintain at least 8 physician-led clinic days/week |
| Foot & Ankle | 2 | 6 | 4 | Maintain at least 4 physician-led clinic days/week |
| Hand | 2 | 6 | 4 | Maintain at least 4 physician-led clinic days/week |
| Pain Management | 5 | 15 | 10 | Maintain at least 10 physician-led clinic days/week |

### Clinic Capacity Calculation

For purposes of PTO planning, each full-time physician is currently assumed to
contribute three (3) physician clinic days per week to the service line's total
clinic capacity.

**Formula:** Total Full-Time Physicians × 3 Clinic Days per Physician × 66% =
Minimum Required Physician Clinic Capacity

When an additional full-time physician is added to a service line, the required
clinic capacity will be recalculated using this formula. The result may be rounded as
operationally appropriate to establish a practical whole or partial clinic-day
requirement.

**Example:** 7 physicians × 3 clinic days = 21 total physician clinic days;
21 × 66% = 13.86. The service line should therefore maintain approximately 14
physician clinic days per week during periods of physician PTO.

## 16-Week Physician PTO and Clinic Coverage Audit

> **Audit Requirement:** The Director of Clinical Scheduling will maintain a rolling
> 16-week review of physician PTO and clinic schedules. Any anticipated coverage
> deficiency will be escalated to the VP of Operations, who will work with the
> affected physicians on a coverage plan. Coverage options may include physicians who
> are not on PTO adding additional clinic days or sessions. If concerns remain, the
> VP of Operations will escalate the matter to the appropriate MEC leader for
> additional review.

The Director of Clinical Scheduling will conduct a rolling audit of the physician PTO
and clinic schedules a minimum of sixteen (16) weeks in advance to proactively
identify potential physician coverage concerns.

The purpose of this audit is to ensure that approved and pending physician PTO does
not reduce physician-led clinic availability below the minimum service-line
requirements established in this policy and that adequate new-patient access is
maintained.

During the 16-week audit, the Director of Clinical Scheduling will:

1. Identify all approved and pending physician PTO occurring within the upcoming
   16-week scheduling period.
2. Review overlapping physician absences within each service line.
3. Evaluate the anticipated physician-led clinic capacity during the affected week(s).
4. Confirm that the applicable service line will maintain the required minimum
   physician clinic coverage, generally 66% or greater of the service line's standard
   physician clinic capacity.
5. Evaluate whether sufficient new-patient appointment availability will remain
   available during the affected period.
6. Identify any week in which physician PTO is expected to create a clinic coverage
   or patient-access concern.
7. Notify the VP of Operations of any identified or anticipated coverage deficiency.

### Coverage Deficiency Escalation and Resolution

| Step | Responsible Party | Required Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1. 16-Week Audit | Director of Clinical Scheduling | Review physician PTO, overlapping absences, physician-led clinic capacity, and new-patient access for each service line. |
| 2. Coverage Validation | Director of Clinical Scheduling | Confirm that the service line maintains the required minimum physician-led clinic capacity. |
| 3. Coverage Concern Identified | Director of Clinical Scheduling | Notify the VP of Operations when anticipated PTO results in, or is expected to result in, insufficient physician clinic coverage. |
| 4. Physician Discussion | VP of Operations | Discuss the identified coverage concern with the affected physician(s) and evaluate available options to maintain required clinic capacity. |
| 5. Coverage Mitigation | VP of Operations / Physicians | Develop a coverage plan, which may include physicians who are not on PTO adding additional clinic days or clinic sessions, modifying clinic schedules, redistributing clinic capacity, or adjusting requested PTO dates. |
| 6. Additional Leadership Review | VP of Operations / MEC Leader | If adequate coverage cannot be established or additional concerns remain, the VP of Operations will escalate the matter to the appropriate MEC leader for review and discussion. |
| 7. Final Coverage Plan | VP of Operations / Applicable Leadership | Confirm the final PTO and clinic coverage plan before the affected scheduling period. |

### Coverage Options

The organization will make reasonable efforts to resolve identified coverage
deficiencies before modifying or denying physician PTO. Available options may include:

- Physicians who are not on PTO adding additional clinic days or clinic sessions
  during the affected week;
- Redistributing clinic sessions among available physicians within the service line;
- Adjusting physician administrative or other non-clinic time, when operationally
  appropriate, to create additional clinic capacity;
- Modifying clinic templates to protect new-patient appointment availability;
- Coordinating alternative PTO dates when adequate coverage cannot otherwise be
  achieved; or
- Denying or modifying a PTO request when the required physician-led clinic coverage
  cannot be maintained.

PA or other advanced practice provider coverage may support clinic operations but
will not substitute for the required physician-led clinic capacity. A physician must
be present and conducting the clinic for that clinic time to count toward the service
line's minimum physician clinic requirement.

## Multiple Physicians Requesting PTO

The number of physicians who may be off at the same time is not determined solely by
physician headcount. Approval is dependent upon whether the remaining physicians can
maintain the minimum required physician-led clinic capacity.

When two or more physicians request overlapping PTO, Scheduling and Operations will
review the anticipated clinic schedule before approving the requests. If the minimum
required clinic capacity would not otherwise be maintained, the organization may
require additional clinic coverage by physicians who are not on PTO, redistribute
clinic sessions, adjust PTO dates, or deny one or more PTO requests when adequate
coverage cannot reasonably be established.

## PTO Review and Escalation

When a PTO request cannot be approved because the minimum clinic-coverage
requirements are not met, the Director of Clinical Scheduling will communicate the
denial and the operational reason for the decision to the physician.

Any physician who has questions or concerns regarding a PTO denial may request
further review. The VP of Operations will review the operational circumstances with
the physician and, when appropriate, include the applicable service-line leader
and/or MEC leader in the discussion. The final determination will consider patient
access, physician clinic coverage, operational requirements, and the circumstances of
the PTO request.

## Ongoing Accountability

The 16-week review is intended to function as a rolling audit. The Director of
Clinical Scheduling is responsible for continuing to monitor the schedule as PTO
requests are added, modified, or canceled.

Approval of PTO does not eliminate the requirement to maintain appropriate physician
clinic coverage. If subsequent schedule changes create a coverage deficiency, the
Director of Clinical Scheduling will notify the VP of Operations so that a coverage
plan can be established with the affected physicians and, when necessary, the
appropriate MEC leader.

## Guiding Principle for PTO Approval

The objective of this policy is not to unnecessarily restrict physician PTO. Rather,
it is to balance physician time away from work with the organization's responsibility
to maintain patient access and continuity of care.

Whenever feasible, the organization will work with physicians to accommodate
requested PTO through proactive planning and redistribution of clinic coverage.
However, maintaining sufficient physician-led clinic availability and new-patient
access will take priority when requested PTO dates create an operational coverage
conflict.
