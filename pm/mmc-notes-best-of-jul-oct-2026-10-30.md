## What the useful notes have in common

Almost all of the 49 record something the practice said, and most say who said it — a first name, sometimes a role. The stronger ones also capture a change (a provider gone, a merger, a move, a lunch rule) or end with a scheduled next step (three carry a calendar date, two a “next week” or a standing call-back), and those are the easiest to act on. Across the 988 entries since July, 100 contain a bare line like “Immediate spine availability” or “dropped off cards” (10% of the set) and 45 of those say nothing else the practice said; they confirm a stop was made and little else. The content categories are rarer: 104 entries carry a referral-intent statement, 153 a blocker or system rule, 57 a practice change, 19 a competitor mention, 19 a payer statement (the practice's mix or our own payer line).

**Three things to do with this list.** (1) The practice changes in section 4 are MMC record edits and route decisions, most of which can be made this week (two of the notes say the record edits were already done). (2) The open items in the status table (an insurance list, a shadowing request, a point-of-contact request, two scheduled lunches to confirm, a provider route at The Family Doctor) each need an owner and a follow-up date. (3) The system-rule notes in section 2 are tiering evidence: a Henry Ford or Corewell site that says it refers inside the system belongs lower in the book than its name suggests, whatever its history.

**Cell numbers.** Several notes record exchanging cell numbers with an office (Berkley Urgent Care, Midwest Spine and Sports Group, Blue Water Primary Care). That is fine for scheduling contact; patient details should go through the referral channel, not by text — worth a line at the huddle.

**Caveat.** Notes are attributed to the company's current owner; a few are a colleague's note about the account. Thank-you lines that list referred patients by name were the most common privacy issue in the set — a team habit to change at the huddle: thank the office, not the patient, in the note.
