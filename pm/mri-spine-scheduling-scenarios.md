# MRI × Spine Scheduling — Training & QA Scenarios

**Companion to:** the MRI Scheduling Talking Points (Situations #1–#2) and
`pm/spine-imaging-pain-session-outcomes.md` (the 2026-07-22 decisions).
**For:** Kelley's call-center training + coaching (the reviewed-call follow-up) and QA scoring.
**Status: DRAFT for Kelley to finalize wording.** These are **composite, fictional scenarios**
built from real call *patterns* — no patient names, numbers, or PHI.

> **Decisions these scenarios encode:**
> - **Spine-first** for neck/back symptoms (numbness, tingling, pain down an arm or leg, back
>   pain, back-injection requests) → lead with a Spine provider.
> - **No-order MRI** → offer a Spine consult first; if declined, offer the self-pay MRI and
>   transfer to the MRI team with a voicemail heads-up.
> - **Self-pay price → don't quote.** Reps say the option exists and hand off to the MRI/billing
>   team for the actual number.
> - **Insurance → the MRI team verifies** facility participation; reps don't quote it.
> - **Acute/severe symptoms → urgent path**, never a routine MRI or a future slot.
> - **MRI scheduling by a rep = "it depends"** (Joe/Kelley to finalize the boundary). Below,
>   each order-in-hand scenario shows **both** branches: *comfortable + straightforward →
>   rep may schedule*; *otherwise → warm-transfer to MRI*. Either branch: **capture name +
>   number first**, so the patient is never lost.

## How to use these
- **Training:** walk a new rep through Core Flows 1–5 first, then the Edge Cases.
- **Coaching:** pick the scenario closest to a reviewed call; ask "what did the flow call for
  here?" before giving the answer. (The agent instinct we want to protect: get spine patients
  to Spine — we're only tuning *how* the hand-off happens.)
- **QA:** score against the "Ideal handling" bullets. The recurring must-haves are the same in
  every scenario: **recognize spine → offer the right door → capture name + number → hand off
  cleanly → log the referral source.**

## Core flows

### Scenario 1 — Outside order in hand, ordered by a PCP · (S#1)
**Caller:** "My primary doctor ordered an MRI of my knee — I'd like to get it scheduled with you."
**Ideal handling:**
- Confirm who ordered it (PCP) and capture name + callback number.
- Offer a follow-up with one of our Ortho/Spine providers to review the results — booked **≥3
  days after** the MRI so results are ready. (This is the funnel — offer it even if they only
  called for the scan.)
- For the MRI itself: *if the order + prior auth are in hand and the rep is comfortable* → may
  schedule; *otherwise* → warm-transfer to the MRI team (voicemail heads-up). Either way, the
  **MRI team verifies facility insurance** — reps don't quote participation.
- If they decline the follow-up: invite them to call back if results point to seeing a specialist.
**Coaching note:** the results follow-up is how a PCP-ordered scan becomes one of our patients.
Don't skip it.

### Scenario 2 — Outside order in hand, ordered by an outside specialist · (S#1, the missing branch)
**Caller:** "I saw an orthopedic doctor at another practice, they ordered a shoulder MRI, and I
can't get in there for weeks — can I come to you instead?"
**Ideal handling:**
- A specialist's order is appropriate as written → proceed on the order + prior auth.
- Capture name + number **and the referring practice** (this is referral intelligence — it
  feeds the liaison team).
- Schedule-or-transfer per the "it depends" branch; MRI team verifies facility insurance.
- Note the urgency (they're leaving a competitor's wait) — move fast, don't let it sit.
**Coaching note:** this is demand defecting *to* us. The fastest way to lose it is a cold
voicemail — capture contact first.

### Scenario 3 — No order, spine symptoms, accepts the consult · (S#2 + D1)
**Caller:** "Urgent care told me I'll probably need an MRI — my lower back is killing me and
it's shooting down my leg."
**Ideal handling:**
- Recognize spine symptoms (radiating leg pain) → **Spine-first:** "The fastest way to the
  right scan is to start with one of our Spine providers — they'll evaluate you, order exactly
  the right MRI, and it all happens here under one roof. Can I set that up?"
- Book the Spine evaluation. **No MRI is scheduled** — the provider orders the correct study.
- Capture name + number; log who referred them (urgent care).
**Coaching note:** back pain + radiating leg symptoms = think Spine. Lead with the provider,
not the scan.

### Scenario 4 — No order, spine symptoms, declines the consult → self-pay · (S#2 + D2)
**Caller:** "I don't want an appointment, I just want the MRI."
**Ideal handling:**
- Explain, plainly: an MRI can't be scheduled without a provider's order — the one exception is
  the **self-pay** option.
- Offer self-pay. **Don't quote a price** — "there's a self-pay MRI option; our MRI/billing
  team handles the exact pricing. Let me get you to them." Capture name + number **before**
  transferring; give the voicemail heads-up + 24-hour callback.
- Offer a results follow-up with our provider once the MRI is done.
**Coaching note:** two must-nots — don't quote a number from memory, and don't transfer before
you've captured name + number.

### Scenario 5 — Cash-pay price shopper · (D2 + "don't quote")
**Caller:** "How much is an MRI if I pay cash?"
**Ideal handling:**
- "We do have a self-pay MRI option — the exact price is handled by our MRI/billing team. I can
  connect you, or take your name and number for a callback." **No number quoted.**
- Offer the value-add: "If you'd want it reviewed and acted on, starting with a Spine provider
  keeps everything under one roof." Capture name + number.
**Coaching note:** a price shopper is a real buyer. Capture contact and offer the consult —
never quote a price that might be wrong.

## Edge cases / judgment calls

### Scenario 6 — Caller self-routes to the wrong door · (D1 — the heart of it)
**Caller:** "I need a pain-management appointment for my back," *or* "I think I need a foot
doctor — my leg's been numb and tingly."
**Ideal handling:**
- Hear the spine signal (back pain / numbness / tingling / radiating) → **offer the Spine
  provider first:** "It sounds like our Spine team is the right place to start — they handle
  exactly this." Book Spine if appropriate; capture name + number + source.
**Coaching note:** this is the whole point of Spine-first — don't book where the caller *guessed*
they needed to go if the symptoms say Spine.

### Scenario 7 — Acute / red-flag symptoms · (urgent-path line)
**Caller:** "The weakness came on fast and I can barely walk," *or* new severe/worsening symptoms.
**Ideal handling:**
- **Do not** book a routine MRI or a future appointment. Escalate per current urgent policy.
- (This line gets refined once Katie finalizes the escalation path; until then, follow current
  urgent guidance — don't route acute symptoms into a future slot.)
**Coaching note:** sudden weakness / can't-walk / rapidly worsening = urgent, not a future date.

### Scenario 8 — Medicaid or possibly non-covered · (compliance)
**Caller:** "I have Medicaid — can I get an MRI / be seen?"
**Ideal handling:**
- **Never** flat-decline. For the consult, coverage is verified **per provider** (Medicaid
  varies by provider). For an MRI, the **MRI team verifies facility participation**.
- If not covered, offer the self-pay / financing route — don't dead-end.
- Offer an interpreter if there's any language barrier.
**Coaching note:** "we don't take Medicaid" is inaccurate and off-limits — verify per provider,
and always leave the patient with a path.

### Scenario 9 — "Please don't bounce me around" · (the fragile hand-off)
**Caller:** "I've already been transferred twice and left a voicemail — please don't do that again."
**Ideal handling:**
- Empathize; **capture name + number first** so they can't be lost regardless of what happens next.
- *If comfortable and it's straightforward* → assist directly; *otherwise* → warm-transfer with
  a genuine heads-up ("you may reach their voicemail — leave your name and number and they'll
  call back within 24 hours"). If unsure how to help, loop in your Lead.
- Never a cold dump into voicemail.
**Coaching note:** capturing contact up front is the cheapest insurance we have — it protects
the patient even when the transfer hiccups.

## The gold-standard hand-off (say it this way, every time)
> "Before I transfer you, let me grab your name and best callback number… Thanks. I'm sending
> you to our MRI team. You may reach their voicemail — just leave your name and number and
> they'll call you back within 24 hours. And I've got your info here too, so you won't fall
> through the cracks."

*(Ties directly to Mitch's voicemail-greeting + callback follow-up. If the mailbox isn't live
or the callback window can't be held, the promise shouldn't be made — flag to the MRI team.)*
