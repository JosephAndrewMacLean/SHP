### A3. Three things only a physician can do for the referral relationship

1. **Notes back, every visit, to the named referring clinician.** The single most common thing referring offices tell the liaisons is that they want to know what happened to their patient (Macomb Family Medical Center's only objection to sending more was the closed loop). The liaison can promise it; only the clinic can deliver it. If a sender on your list above is on the 'went quiet' line, the first question is whether they got notes.

2. **Doctor-to-doctor contact with your top three.** A short call or a signed note from the physician who actually saw their patients — *thank you, here is my direct line for anything pending* — to the practices at the top of your list. The liaisons visit these offices every two weeks; your name on the relationship is the part they cannot supply. No gifts, meals or anything of value — the contact itself is the point (AKS/Stark).

3. **Tell us which of you sees the telehealth intakes.** 308 B2B new patients came through telehealth/auto intake this year; the sheet names the first doctor seen for only 58 of them. For the other 250 we cannot connect the referring attorney or clinic to the physician who treated the patient, so the thank-you and the notes-back loop stop at the intake desk. One extra column on the weekly sheet (the 'first doctor visit after telehealth' Kristen already keeps) closes it.

## Part B — what the liaisons carry into the referring physician's office

The script is unchanged (`pm/pl-visit-messages.md`: THANK → OPEN → MESSAGE → ASK, no counts out loud). What this year's data adds is *context the liaison can use* and a few facts they can state when asked. Everything below stays inside the existing compliance line: thank the provider for their own patients only, never in earshot of a waiting room; no gifts or inducements; no outcome or superiority claims; attorney conversations are service-only.

1. **Put a physician's name on the relationship.** The stop card now says which Synergy doctor the practice's patients actually see (the CSV above). *"Your patients have been seeing Dr. McCarty — here is his direct line if anything is pending."* That turns a facility relationship into a doctor-to-doctor one, and it is the honest answer to the most common OPEN question (what happens to my patient?).

2. **Ask the loop question first.** *"Are you getting our notes back after each visit?"* — before the pitch. Where the answer is no, that is the fix to carry home; where the answer is yes, that is the thing to thank them for continuing.

3. **Facts the liaison may state if asked (data-true, not clinical claims):** spine referrals from community practices have grown every quarter this year; a referred back/neck patient is seen by the spine team the same week (the existing visit-script claim, itself pending compliance review); the practice's referrals go to a named spine physician who sends notes back. *Do not* quote the practice's own counts aloud (Kristen's rule) and *do not* compare Synergy to anyone else.

4. **For the accounts that went quiet** (Part A per-doctor lists; the relationship workbook's Protect tab): lead with ownership — *"We owe you a visit — that's on us"* — then THANK → OPEN. If the physician has already called, say so; if not, offer it: *"Dr. Salar would like to call you directly — when is a bad time?"*

5. **For first-time senders since July** (Part A 'new since July 1'): *"How was that first experience? Anything we should do differently next time?"* — the ask is the second patient, not a program.

6. **For the chiropractors and the orthopedic groups without spine surgeons** — the two highest-converting relationship types in the data — the co-management line is the one that works: the patient stays theirs; our spine team handles the spine problem and sends the patient back with notes. The relationship workbook lists 80 chiropractors and 23 ortho groups in MMC that nobody is visiting yet.

7. **Attorneys** (phone cadence, Kristen): nearly all attorney spine arrives through the telehealth intake, so the physician who sees those patients is rarely visible to the firm. Service-only language as written; the one new fact is a name — which spine physician their clients are seeing — once A3 item 3 is in place.
