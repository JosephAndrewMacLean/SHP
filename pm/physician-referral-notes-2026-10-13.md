Tell the liaison team which accounts you have contacted and whether your direct line may be shared — nothing is said in your name until you say so. ## Part B — what the liaisons carry into the referring physician's office

The script is unchanged (`pm/pl-visit-messages.md`: THANK → OPEN → MESSAGE → ASK, no counts out loud). What this year's data adds is *context the liaison can use* and a few facts they can state when asked. Everything below stays inside the existing compliance line: thank the provider for their own patients only, never in earshot of a waiting room; no gifts or inducements; no outcome or superiority claims; attorney conversations are service-only.

1. **Put a physician's name on the relationship.** The stop card can say which Synergy doctor the practice's patients actually see (the CSV above, month-level dates only). *"Your patients have been seeing Dr. McCarty."* — and, once that physician has opted in, *"here is the direct line if anything is pending."* That turns a facility relationship into a doctor-to-doctor one, and it is the honest answer when an office asks what happens to its patient.

2. **Make the loop question the first OPEN with clinical referrers** (after the THANK, as the script orders it). *"Are you getting our notes back after each visit?"* — before any message. Where the answer is no, that is the fix to carry home; where the answer is yes, that is the thing to thank them for continuing. The attorney OPEN stays the records / scheduling / communication service check as written — clinical notes go to the referring clinician, not to a firm.

3. **What the liaison may say, and what stays off the script.** May say: the practice's referrals are seen by a named spine physician (and, once that physician has opted in, that notes come back to the referring clinician after every visit); a referred back/neck patient is seen the same week when a slot is available — the existing visit-script claim, with its guardrails intact (soonest real slot, never a blanket guarantee; HMO plans need the referral paperwork we handle with the staff; Medicaid-likely accounts are verified before any access promise; pending compliance review). Stays off: counts, trends and 'this year' (Kristen's rule — numbers out loud sound transactional), and any comparison with another practice or system. The growth story in Part A is for the physicians, not for the office.

4. **For the accounts that went quiet for the whole group** (the 'quiet for the whole group' names in Part A's 'Suggested this month' lines): lead with ownership — *"We owe you a visit — that's on us"* — then THANK → OPEN. A practice that still sends to a colleague or another service line gets the ordinary THANK, not an apology. If the physician has already called, say so; if the physician has agreed to call, offer it: *"Dr. Salar would like to call you directly — when would be a good time?"* Nothing is offered in a physician's name before they have said yes.

5. **For practices new to the spine group since July** (Part A 'first spine patient to you' lines, the group-new ones): the script's Convert line, verbatim — *"How was that experience? Make us your default for the next back/neck patient."* — the ask is the second patient, not a program.

6. **For the chiropractors and the orthopedic-tagged practices** — the two highest-converting relationship types in the data — the co-management line is the one the script uses: the patient stays theirs; our spine team handles just the spine problem and sends the patient back with notes. The relationship workbook lists 80 chiropractors and 23 ortho-tagged practices in MMC that are in nobody's book and sent nothing this year (40 and 16 of them unvisited since March).

7. **Attorneys** (phone cadence, Kristen): 91 of the 95 attorney spine referrals arrived through the telehealth intake, so our sheet rarely shows which physician saw them. Service-only language as written (every referral is evaluated on clinical merit); the one new fact is a name — which spine physician their clients are seeing — once A3 item 3 is in place and only for physicians who have opted in to being named to law firms.
