# New-patient tracker — rows the record does not support on its own

**Date:** 2026-10-29 · **Status:** internal working file for Joe; signals about rows and lists, not a scorecard. Nothing here is a finding about a person: every class is a mechanical rule on the row and the MMC export, and what a row actually was is a question for whoever coded it. · **Source:** `2025-2026 PL NPT AUTO MASTER 26.xlsx` as uploaded Oct 29 — 101 weekly sheets (appointments 2025-01-02 → 2026-10-23), 10,519 patient rows, the AUTO sheet (381 rows) and the quarterly total sheets — cross-referenced with the Map My Customer (MMC) All Activity export of Oct 29 (visits only; calls are not in that export) and the referrer-to-practice map in `pm/b2b-referrer-company-2026.csv`. **Row-level results are in a workbook sent to Joe directly and deliberately not committed:** its rows carry appointment dates. No patient name or chart number appears there or here; each flagged row is pointed at by sheet name and Excel row number in the master file.

## 1. How the sheet counts, as far as it can be reverse-engineered

Each weekly sheet lists new-patient appointments with a liaison code in the Mrkt Comments column (KJ Kristen · JJ Jasmine · CO Coty · KG Kessia · SS Sean · JP Jim). 9,605 of the 10,519 rows carry a code: Kristen 4,927 · Jasmine 2,049 · Coty 1,221 · Kessia 889 · Sean 274 · Jim 245. From mid-2025 a Channel column records what the patient said on the call (B2B, B2C, Natural, No call, Not asked).

The count is encoded in where a row sits. From the week of 7.7.25, 70 sheets split their rows: the rows listed under a **"B2B RESULTS"** header are the week's liaison-credited new patients and are what the per-liaison block counts; rows above the header are consumer-channel rows that may still carry a code (the sheet's "Overlap" line — ten sheets from Apr–Jun 2025 mark 157 such rows with an "X" instead); and from April 2026 rows parked *below* the summary block are the week's telehealth intakes, which are not counted that week. On that reading 5,312 rows are inside the counting rule (on the sheets without the header the fallback is: plain code, Channel B2B or blank, not below the block), and 3,183 coded rows with a consumer channel sit outside it.

The quarterly sheets carry a confirmed weekly count per liaison ("PL CONFIRMED") for every sheet-week from April 2025 to October 2026 — 91 weeks, 455 week-by-liaison pairs. Against it, the placement rule reproduces 89% of the pairs exactly and 96% within one (89% and 96% once same-sheet duplicates are removed); the channel column alone would give 81%. The rest runs both ways (26 pairs where the confirmed count is higher than the rows, 26 lower). In total the confirmed counts come to 4,512 against 4,515 counted rows (4,495 without same-sheet duplicates) — **within about 0.4% in total and within 2% for any one liaison, 4 of 6 on the high side** (Kristen +6 (+0.3%) · Jasmine +4 (+0.4%) · Coty +0 (+0.0%) · Kessia +5 (+1.1%) · Sean +2 (+1.9%) · Jim +0 (+0.0%)). So the reported totals track the rows; the question is which rows carry a code at all. Jan–Mar 2025 has no confirmed breakdown and was not compared. The rule is written nowhere in the file (the "revised" and "confirmed" sheet titles show it is applied by hand), one sheet (8.10.26) labels its Channel column "Sts", and the five January 2025 sheets have no summary block at all. Monthly totals ran between 82% and 118% of the budget line on the quarterly sheets (the annual "2026 TL" sheet carries slightly different May and June 2026 figures from the Q2 2026 sheet; the quarterly sheets are used here). Sheets 9.28.26 to 10.12.26 already carry an MMC last-visit column (171 rows filled; it matches the export on most comparable rows and is honoured in the visit test below).

**G is the one structural thing in the file, and it is a listing habit rather than a double count.** 297 credited chart numbers appear on two or more credited rows; the telehealth pattern produces 260 second rows (258 chart numbers): 256 are the telehealth intake re-listed in the week the in-person appointment lands, 4 are the in-person appointment itself listed as a new-patient row. The first listing sits below the summary block (not counted) on 233 of them and is inside the counting rule on only 27; the second listing sits under the B2B RESULTS header on 248 and is counted on 260. So the patient is counted once, in the in-person week — but the second row carries the original telehealth date, so anyone counting rows by appointment date, or reading the row-level file, sees the patient twice and dates the credit to the wrong week. Checked against the AUTO sheet, 254 of the 256 re-listed rows have an in-person date there and 251 of those land in the week they were re-listed in.

## 5. What would make this unnecessary next time

1. **Write the counting rule down** on the sheet (rows under B2B RESULTS count; consumer-channel rows and telehealth intakes do not) so the blocks can be rebuilt by formula — and label the Channel column the same way on every sheet.
2. **List the telehealth intake once.** Today it is parked below the block in one week and re-listed with its old date in another; give it one row, dated to the appointment that counts, and let the AUTO sheet track the pair.
3. **No code without a provider:** "Other", unspecified urgent care and "Social Media" rows stay uncoded until a provider is captured; a liaison-sourced patient gets its own marker rather than the liaison's name in the referrer field.
4. **Credit needs a touch on record before the appointment** — a visit or a logged call in MMC. Where the account is phone-worked, log the call (the same Call-vs-Visit rule proposed in the Oct 29 check-in review). The MMC last-visit column that sheets 9.28.26 to 10.12.26 carry is the right instrument; carry it on every sheet.
5. **Rows naming a Synergy clinician as the referrer need a source decision** before they count toward anyone.
6. **Dedupe within the sheet** before the block is filled, fix the malformed dates, and give the 75 uncredited B2B rows a second look — if the patient named a provider, someone owns that relationship.

Caveats: the MMC export carries visits only, so a phone-only relationship reads as "no visit on record"; the referrer map covers 93% of credited rows (exact map, first-and-last-name matching against MMC contacts with first-name variants, MMC records at the same street address, and same-organisation records in the same city); the chart-age estimate assumes sequential chart numbers at the observed rate of about 60 a day. **Draft for Joe's review; nothing here is a finding about a person, no patient identifiers are included, and the row-level workbook stays inside the PL program.**
