# New-patient tracker — rows the record does not support on its own

**Date:** 2026-10-29 · **Status:** internal working file for Joe; signals about rows and lists, not a scorecard. Nothing here is a finding about a person: every class is a mechanical rule on the row and the MMC export, and what a row actually was is a question for whoever coded it. · **Source:** `2025-2026 PL NPT AUTO MASTER 26.xlsx` as uploaded Oct 29 — 101 weekly sheets (appointments 2025-01-02 → 2026-10-23), 10,599 patient rows, the AUTO sheet (381 rows) and the quarterly total sheets — cross-referenced with the Map My Customer (MMC) All Activity export of Oct 29 (visits only; calls are not in that export) and the referrer-to-practice map in `pm/b2b-referrer-company-2026.csv`. **Row-level results are in a workbook sent to Joe directly and deliberately not committed:** its rows carry appointment dates. No patient name or chart number appears there or here; each flagged row is pointed at by sheet name and Excel row number in the master file.

## 1. How the sheet counts, as far as it can be reverse-engineered

Each weekly sheet lists new-patient appointments with a liaison code in the Mrkt Comments column (KJ Kristen · JJ Jasmine · CO Coty · KG Kessia · SS Sean · JP Jim). 9,605 of the 10,599 rows carry a code: Kristen 4,927 · Jasmine 2,049 · Coty 1,221 · Kessia 889 · Sean 274 · Jim 245. From mid-2025 a Channel column records what the patient said on the call (B2B, B2C, Natural, No call, Not asked), and the summary block under each sheet reports a count per liaison plus B2B / B2C / Natural / Undetermined lines.

The per-liaison block was reproduced best by one rule: **code present, no "?" or qualifier, and Channel either B2B or blank** (5,663 rows). Rows with a code but a consumer channel (3,183 rows) are not counted — the sheet's "Overlap" line is for them (its value cell is blank, but ten sheets from Apr–Jun 2025 mark 161 such rows with an "X"). Against the block on each sheet, 80% of the 451 sheet-by-liaison pairs match the rule exactly as it stands; after the telehealth rows listed again and the same-sheet duplicates (§3) are taken out, 78% match exactly and 92% are within one, with the rest running both ways (43 pairs where the block is higher than the adjusted rows, 58 lower). Nine month-end sheets carry two or three blocks (partial-month blocks, a week total, or a revised block after rows were appended); the block covering each sheet's own rows was chosen by hand and those nine are the least reliable comparisons. The quarterly sheets tell the same story: 5,197 credited new patients reported for Jan 2025 → Sep 2026 against 5,395 rows inside the rule by appointment month and 5,118 adjusted — **within about 1.5% in total and within 6% for any one liaison, 5 of 6 on the high side** (Kristen +8 (+0.3%) · Jasmine +39 (+3.7%) · Coty +18 (+3.3%) · Kessia +9 (+1.7%) · Sean +6 (+5.8%) · Jim -1 (-0.5%)). So the reported totals track the rows; the question is which rows carry a code at all. The rule is not written anywhere in the file, the "revised" and "confirmed" sheet titles show it is applied by hand, and one sheet (8.10.26) labels its Channel column "Sts", which any formula-based rebuild would trip over. Monthly totals ran between 82% and 118% of the budget line on the quarterly sheets (the annual "2026 TL" sheet carries slightly different May and June 2026 figures from the Q2 2026 sheet; the quarterly sheets are used here). The four most recent sheets already carry an MMC last-visit column (171 rows filled); it agrees with the export and is honoured in the visit test below.

## 5. What would make this unnecessary next time

1. **Write the counting rule down** on the sheet (code + B2B-or-blank channel; telehealth rows listed again and same-sheet duplicates excluded) so the blocks can be rebuilt by formula — and label the Channel column the same way on every sheet.
2. **One row per patient per episode:** the telehealth intake and the in-person appointment are one new patient; keep the AUTO sheet as the second-appointment tracker, not a second NPT row.
3. **No code without a provider:** "Other", unspecified urgent care and "Social Media" rows stay uncoded until a provider is captured; a liaison-sourced patient gets its own marker rather than the liaison's name in the referrer field.
4. **Credit needs a touch on record before the appointment** — a visit or a logged call in MMC. Where the account is phone-worked, log the call (the same Call-vs-Visit rule proposed in the Oct 29 check-in review). The MMC last-visit column that the four most recent sheets already carry is the right instrument; carry it on every sheet.
5. **Rows naming a Synergy clinician as the referrer need a source decision** before they count toward anyone.
6. **Dedupe within the sheet** before the block is filled, fix the malformed dates, and give the 75 uncredited B2B rows a second look — if the patient named a provider, someone owns that relationship.

Caveats: the MMC export carries visits only, so a phone-only relationship reads as "no visit on record"; the referrer map covers 93% of credited rows (exact map, first-and-last-name matching against MMC contacts, MMC records at the same street address, and same-organisation records in the same city); the chart-age estimate assumes sequential chart numbers at the observed rate of about 60 a day. **Draft for Joe's review; nothing here is a finding about a person, no patient identifiers are included, and the row-level workbook stays inside the PL program.**
