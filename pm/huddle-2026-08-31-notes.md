### Risk from the Labor Day booking hole — post-meeting analysis, not available in the room

Joe's own rule is that roughly 80% of new patients book within seven days of the appointment date, so a week's fill is created in the seven days before it. Run that against the calendar. Labor Day, Monday Sep 7, sits inside the booking window of every appointment day from **Sep 8 through Sep 14**. Four of those five days — Sep 8, 9, 10 and 11 — are in the four-day week itself; only Sep 14 is in the week after. So roughly four fifths of the Labor Day exposure lands on the four-day week, and about one fifth on the Sep 14 week.

That also corrects how the two windows were described in the room. Appointments in the week of Sep 7–11 are booked mostly during **Sep 1 – Sep 11**, a window that itself contains Labor Day — so the four-day week has a capacity problem *and* a demand problem, not capacity alone. The week of Sep 14–18 — the one being promised at 130 ortho + 68 spine = 198 new patients — is booked mostly during Sep 7–13, a window that **opens on Labor Day**.

In GA4, Monday is the single biggest weekday demand day on all four measures (23.2%–27.8% of Mon–Fri volume, 2026-07-01 to 2026-08-30; three of the four sit between 23.2% and 23.5%, and only click_to_call reaches 27.8%). On past holiday Mondays the phones ran **71%–87% below** comparable Mondays, measured across three holidays — Memorial Day 2026, Labor Day 2025 and Labor Day 2024. On the **one** holiday Monday with usable web data — Memorial Day 2026, the same month the web event stabilised — the web form ran about **39% below**. That web number is a single observation, not a pattern. The Tuesday after Labor Day 2025 recovered only +12% against other Tuesdays, so the lost Monday is not made up later.

*Modelled, not measured:* for the week of Sep 14–18, only the Sep 14 appointment day has Labor Day inside its own booking window — the windows for Sep 15, 16, 17 and 18 each contain Monday Sep 14, a normal Monday. So 198 new patients × ~80% booked in the prior seven days = 158.4, ÷ 5 appointment days, × Monday's ~23% share of weekday demand ≈ **7–8 new patients gross**, and roughly **4–6** once the measured partial-loss rates above are applied, since neither channel goes fully dark. Bookings for the Sep 15–18 appointment days run through Sep 17, so recovery is not confined to Sep 8–11.

Two assumptions to name, both of which push the modelled number around. Monday's share of *total* new patients is proxied by its share of *web* demand signal; referral and direct phone volume may follow a different weekday curve. And a Mon–Fri share is being applied to a seven-day window that also contains a weekend, which overstates Monday's weight — click_to_call is 27.8% of Mon–Fri but 26.9% of all seven days.

*The whole hole, not just the Sep 14 slice.* The same 7–8 gross per appointment day applies to each of the five clinic days inside Labor Day's seven-day reach — Sep 8, 9, 10, 11 and 14. Five clinic days at 7–8 each is about **40 new patients of gross booking demand**, and that is what one dark Monday is worth in total. Roughly four fifths of it — about **30 gross** — lands on the Sep 8–11 appointment days, where it has to be recovered on top of those days' normal load; the remaining fifth is the 7–8 gross, 4–6 after the measured partial-loss rates, on Sep 14. So the ~40 carried in the four-day-week analysis, the lessons-learned page and the follow-up email is the whole hole across both weeks, not a competing estimate of the Sep 14 week's share. Like the 7–8, the 40 assumes the dark Monday books nothing at all — the pessimistic end, since the −39% web reading says neither channel goes fully dark.

*GA4 caveat, which applies to every figure in this section:* GA4 holds no Liine data and cannot report booked or kept new patients. It measures website demand signal only. The clean window for booking-intent events is **61 days (just under 9 weeks), 2026-07-01 to 2026-08-30**.

| **When** | Entered before the planning session, not during it. There is no Monday session on Sep 7 (Labor Day), so the first entry is due at the **Friday Sep 4** session; the Monday cadence resumes Sep 14. Re-checked at the Friday session before each week starts. |
| **Half days** | Counted as recorded and annotated (e.g. Varghese's Friday half day in Sterling Heights), so a half day is never silently read as a full clinic day. |

**Two derived numbers the column computes automatically:**

1. **Clinic days as a percentage of a normal week.** Two different denominators — do not mix them.
   - **Per physician** = that physician's clinic days ÷ *that physician's* normal-week clinic days: **3**, or **2** for David Mendelson. Never divide one physician's days by a line total; Stephen Mendelson's 2 ÷ 17 is a meaningless number.
   - **Per line** = the line's clinic days ÷ the line's normal week: **12 for spine**, **17 for ortho**. Worked example: ortho, week of Sep 7 = 12 ÷ 17 = **70.6%**.
   - The ortho denominator of 17 stands **pending the David Mendelson reconciliation**. If that resolves to 3 clinic days, ortho's normal week is 18 and the same example is 12 ÷ 18 = **66.7%**. Build the field so the denominator is editable, not hard-coded.
2. **New patients per clinic day** = new-patient target (and, once actuals land, new-patient actual) ÷ clinic days. Worked examples: 130 ÷ 17 = **7.65** in a normal ortho week (130 ÷ 18 = **7.22** if the denominator becomes 18); the 105 target on 12 days = **8.75**.

Together these two answer the question that was missing from the meeting until Kelley counted by hand: how much of a normal week do we actually have, and how hard is each remaining day being asked to work.

## 5. What changed about how we run

The meeting used to be about the current week. It is not any more.

- **Monday and Friday planning sessions now cover next week and the week after.** Those are the weeks where a decision still changes the outcome. Gautam: "This week is gone already by Monday morning."
- **There is no Monday session on Sep 7 (Labor Day).** The week of Sep 7–11 is confirmed at the Friday Sep 4 session, and the next Monday session is Sep 14.
- **Wednesday still looks at all three weeks**, but only at new-patient count versus new-patient target. It is a scoreboard check, not a planning session.
- **Friday's job is to confirm everything is in order before the week starts** — not to fix the week that is ending.
- **Capacity is now stated in clinic days, per physician, before slots are discussed.** Slots without clinic days hid the ortho gap until it was counted out loud in this meeting.
- **Stability beats scrambling.** No half days opened at twelve hours' notice, no schedule edits in the new schedule's first live week, no clinic days taken back from physicians. The point of the published schedule is that it is real.
