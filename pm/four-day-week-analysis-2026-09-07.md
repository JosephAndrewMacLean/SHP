Spine loses nothing — 12 clinic days in both weeks — because Kelley has all four spine physicians at three clinic days inside the Tue–Fri week, so Labor Day takes none of them. Ortho loses five clinic days out of seventeen, which is 29.4%, not 20%. The ortho error is large enough to change the target.

### What 105 actually requires

Gautam's capacity standard is 3 clinic days per physician, at least 10 open new-patient slots per clinic day, spread across three separate days. Take him at his own arithmetic and the gap becomes visible.

| Measure | Normal week (17 ortho clinic days) | Week of Sep 7 at the 105 written down | Week of Sep 7 at pro-rata |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ortho clinic days | 17 | 12 | 12 |
| New-patient slots at 10/day | 170 | 120 | 120 |
| New patients | 130 | 105 | 91.8 |
| **New patients per clinic day** | **7.65** | **8.75** | **7.65** |
| **Fill rate against the 10-slot standard** | **76.5%** | **87.5%** | **76.5%** |

The pro-rata column is 91.8. It is carried to the team as 92.

Read the fill-rate row as a **ceiling, not measured fill** — and the same goes for every fill figure in this analysis, spine's 56.7% included. The numerator is a target rather than a delivered count, and the denominator is Gautam's stated *minimum* of at least 10 open new-patient slots per clinic day. Kelley confirmed the week of Sep 14–18 runs "way over" 10, so the real denominators are larger and true fill is lower than every number here. Real slot counts from Kelley would replace all of these.

The 105 written down on the tracker requires 8.75 new patients per clinic day against a normal-week 7.65 — **14% above normal-week productivity**, on the first live week of a new schedule, in a holiday week. Expressed as fill rate, it requires 87.5% of Gautam's own slot standard against a normal 76.5%.

The capacity-consistent number is 130 × 12/17 = 91.8, which rounds to **92**. At 91.8, ortho performs at exactly its normal fill rate — 76.5% — and simply has fewer days to do it in.

**105 is a stretch number. It is not a capacity-consistent one.** The distance between them is 13 patients, or about 1.1 extra new patients on every one of the twelve ortho clinic days.

### The two things that could legitimately close part of that gap

This should be argued honestly in both directions. Two things could push ortho above pro-rata — PA support absorbing follow-ups, and a softer ASC week converting to clinic time. Neither is countable today. Neither is on record as a change anyone has actually made.

**1. PA support.** This sits on Gautam's capacity watchout: line up PA support so PAs absorb follow-ups and physicians see the new patients. If that happens, new patients per clinic day rises — exactly the lever needed here. It is a forward-looking watchout, not a completed action.

*What would have to be true to count it:* ortho clinic days would have to be running out of new-patient slots. Nothing we have says they are. Fill against the 10-slot minimum is **at most** 76.5% in a normal week — a bound, not a measurement, since 130 is a target and 10 slots a day is a floor — and it is lower still wherever slots run "way over" 10, as Kelley says they do for the week of Sep 14–18. Spare new-patient slots therefore exist a fortiori, and adding more slots to days that already have spare ones adds nothing. PA support only converts into new patients if the constraint is physician time consumed by follow-ups, not slot supply. The test is a slot count by ortho clinic day for the week of Sep 7, plus how many ortho clinic days in recent weeks actually exhausted their new-patient slots. Until that count exists, this is a plausible mechanism with no measured size.

**2. A softer ASC week converting to clinic time.** If ASC volume drops next week, physician time could convert to clinic time.

*What would have to be true to count it:* first, note that the record cuts the other way. The four-day week is already short on ASC surgeries, because Monday ASC days are converting into clinic days. Gautam raised this in the meeting as a problem, not as freed-up clinic capacity — "we are basically going to start out the month really short ASC surgeries… next week the ASC team is going to be super [angry]." Second, it would have to be a confirmed schedule change, in writing, before the week starts. Three of the meeting's own rules bear directly on it. No clinic days get stolen. No last-minute capacity — opening half-days at twelve hours' notice does not fill and signals that the published schedule is not real. And nothing on the schedule from the week of Sep 7 onward changes without written email approval from both Joe and Katie, copying Anna and Gautam. Gautam was reluctant to change anything in the new schedule's first live week, on the grounds of the message that sends. So this is not a forecast input. It is a request that would have to be approved by Fri Sep 4 to be real, and Gautam has already signalled he would likely decline it.

Neither item is a reason to forecast 105. Either would be upside against 92.

### Spine is the contrast, and it is the proof the method works

Spine has **12 clinic days in both weeks**. McCarty, Maslak, Varghese and Salar each have three — and each has three inside the four-day week, whose only working days are Tue Sep 8 to Fri Sep 11. Gautam's read in the meeting — a solid spine week and a low ortho week for Sep 7 — is correct on capacity, and for a specific reason the arithmetic now names: spine keeps all twelve of its clinic days and ortho loses five of seventeen. It is not a verdict on spine's demand. ## B. The building blocks: what a clinic day is worth

The planning unit is new patients per clinic day. Clinic days × rate = the week's expected number, calculated before anyone argues about the target.

| Service line | Rate implied by the standard | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| Ortho | **7.65 NP per clinic day** | 130 ÷ 17 clinic days |
| Spine | **5.67 NP per clinic day** | 68 ÷ 12 clinic days |

Applied to the three weeks on the tracker:

| Week | Ortho clinic days | Expected ortho at 7.65 | Ortho target on tracker | Spine clinic days | Expected spine at 5.67 | Spine target on tracker |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aug 31 – Sep 4 | not yet counted — Kelley to backfill | — | 120 | not yet counted | — | 60+ |
| Sep 7 – Sep 11 | 12 | **92** | 105 | 12 | **68** | 68 |
| Sep 14 – Sep 18 | 17 | **130** | 130 | 12 | **68** | 68 |

Read the table for where expectation and target diverge. They agree on spine in both weeks. They agree on ortho in the full week. They disagree on ortho in the four-day week by 13 patients, and that is the entire finding of Section A.

Kelley's backfill of this week's clinic-day counts completes the top row and gives the first week where expected and actual can be compared using the same unit.

### Be honest about what this rate is

The 7.65 and the 5.67 are **derived from the targets, not from observed output**. 130 divided by 17 reproduces 130 when multiplied by 17; 68 divided by 12 reproduces 68. Used that way the arithmetic is circular and proves nothing.

It stops being circular the moment it is applied to a *different* clinic-day count. Twelve ortho clinic days at the rate the 130 standard itself implies gives 92. That number is not an opinion about next week; it is the 130 standard restated on the days that actually exist. Anyone who wants to argue for 105 has to argue that ortho will run 14% above its own standard's productivity, and say why.

What the rate is missing is a measured counterpart: what ortho and spine have actually delivered per clinic day over the recent trailing weeks. That number does not exist in this analysis, and here is why.

### What marketing can and cannot measure

**GA4 holds no Liine data.** This was verified on 2026-07-22 and again in the event scan run for this analysis. GA4 cannot report booked new patients and cannot report kept new patients. It reports website demand signal — clicks, calls initiated, form intent, handoffs — and nothing downstream of them.

Every GA4 number in Section C is therefore a demand-signal number. None of them is a patient count. Where this analysis needs a patient count, it uses the targets and clinic-day counts from the meeting, not GA4.

**This is a named data gap, not a footnote.** The trailing new-patients-per-day baseline Joe committed to bringing into the meeting has to come out of the practice system, not out of GA4. Owner and date are in Section D.

There is a second constraint on the GA4 side. A true trailing thirteen weeks of booking-intent does not exist yet, because the events were not all instrumented that long ago. `new_patient_intent` starts April 2026 at 53 events and only stabilises in May; `book_appointment_click` and `appointment_handoff_to_zocdoc` have clean full-month data only from July 2026. **The clean window is about nine weeks: 2026-07-01 to 2026-08-30, 61 days.** That is what this analysis uses. It is not a whole number of Mon–Fri weeks — 8 Mondays and 8 Tuesdays against 9 each of Wednesday, Thursday and Friday — which matters for every weekday comparison in Section C and is handled there. Only `click_to_call` reaches back further — clean to January 2024 — which is why the multi-year holiday comparisons below are click-to-call only.

## C. The finding: the Labor Day demand hole lands mainly on the four-day week, with about one clinic day of spill into September 14

Everyone in the meeting treated the four-day week as a capacity problem. It is that, and it is also a demand problem. The argument is five steps, each one checkable.

### Step 1 — A week is filled in the seven days before it

Joe's own figure, stated in the meeting: **about 80% of new patients book within seven days of the appointment date.** Applied per appointment date, it runs in both directions. An appointment day is filled from the seven days before it. A lost demand day costs the seven days after it. Everything below depends on applying that one rule the same way to both weeks; applied loosely, the conclusion flips.

### Step 2 — So a lost Monday costs the four days that follow it, plus one day of the week after

Demand that would have been created on Monday Sep 7 reaches appointments from Sep 7 through Sep 14. Sep 7 is the holiday and holds no clinic. The clinic days inside that reach are Tue Sep 8, Wed Sep 9, Thu Sep 10 and Fri Sep 11 — the entire four-day week — plus Mon Sep 14, the first day of the banner week. Four of the five clinic days in reach sit inside the four-day week.

**The four-day week therefore carries two problems, not one.** Capacity: twelve of seventeen ortho clinic days, which is Section A. Demand: its own booking window includes the dark Monday. The window that fills Sep 8–11 runs from Sep 1 through Sep 11 — four normal weekdays, a weekend, Labor Day itself, then the four-day week's own days. Mostly normal, but not entirely.

### Step 3 — The banner week is largely out of the holiday's reach, but not entirely

The week of Sep 14–18 carries 29 clinic days and 198 new patients to fill: 130 ortho plus 68 spine. Appointments on Sep 15, 16, 17 and 18 are filled from Sep 8 onward — all normal days. Labor Day sits outside their seven-day windows entirely. Only Mon Sep 14's window opens on Labor Day.

So the banner week's exposure is about one clinic day of spill, not a whole week. This holds only because the seven-day rule is applied per appointment date. The looser version — "a week is filled in the seven days before the week starts" — would push the whole hole onto the banner week and off the four-day week, and it is a simplification the underlying figure does not support. The distinction is the single most load-bearing assumption in this section.

### Step 4 — Monday is the biggest demand day of the week, on every measure

GA4, weekdays only, 2026-07-01 to 2026-08-30 — about nine weeks, 61 days:

| Event | Mon–Fri total | Monday | Monday share, raw totals | Monday share, per-day averages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| new_patient_intent | 9,039 | 2,122 | 23.5% | **25.0%** |
| book_appointment_click | 8,962 | 2,082 | 23.2% | not available |
| click_to_call | 4,351 | 1,211 | 27.8% | **29.7%** |
| appointment_handoff_to_zocdoc | 3,659 | 856 | 23.4% | not available |

**Read the last column, not the second-to-last.** The window holds 8 Mondays and 8 Tuesdays against 9 each of Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, so raw weekday totals over-weight the back half of the week and understate Monday. Normalising to a per-day average corrects it. The correction runs in favour of the argument here, so the raw shares are the conservative ones; for `book_appointment_click` and `appointment_handoff_to_zocdoc` only the raw share exists, and it is understated by the same mechanism.

Monday is the single biggest weekday on all four measures. It is most extreme on phone calls, which is where the booking actually happens. Per-day averages, click_to_call: Mon 151.4 · Fri 101.7 · Thu 90.9 · Tue 84.3 · Wed 81.4. Per-day averages, new_patient_intent: Mon 265.3 · Tue 264.0 · Thu 200.9 · Fri 167.4 · Wed 165.6.

### Step 5 — On a holiday Monday, the phones go dark, and the week does not get it back

Three separate holiday Mondays, measured:

| Holiday Monday | Measure | Holiday | Comparable Mondays | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Memorial Day 2026-05-25 | click_to_call | 5 | 32 / 31 / 53 (avg 38.7) | **−87%** |
| Memorial Day 2026-05-25 | new_patient_intent | 54 | 70 / 112 / 83 (avg 88.3) | −39% |
| Labor Day 2025-09-01 | click_to_call | 9 | 44 / 51 / 61 / 49 (avg 51.2) | **−82%** |
| Labor Day 2024-09-02 | click_to_call | 7 | 20 / 25 / 32 / 18 (avg 23.8) | **−71%** |

The pattern is consistent across three holiday Mondays spanning 2024, 2025 and 2026: **phones run 71% to 87% dark**, while the web form keeps working at roughly −39%. The channel that converts is the one that stops.

The obvious objection is that the calls simply move to Tuesday. They do not.

**Catch-up test.** The Tuesday after Labor Day 2025 did **41** click-to-calls against an average of **36.8** for the other Tuesdays in the window — **+12%**. A twelve-percent bump on one day does not replace a Monday that lost four fifths of its calls.

**Whole-week test**, click_to_call, Mon–Fri totals:

| Year | Neighbouring weeks | Labor Day week | Gap vs. neighbour average |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | Aug 18-22: 206 · Aug 25-29: 133 · Sep 8-12: 201 · Sep 15-19: 184 (avg 181) | **150** | **−17%** |
| 2024 | Aug 19-23: 84 · Aug 26-30: 69 · Sep 9-13: 83 · Sep 16-20: 56 (avg 73) | **64** | **−12%** |

Two independent years, different absolute volumes, same direction: the holiday week runs **roughly 12–17% below the neighbouring-week average** in both. Treat that as directional, not as a magnitude to plan against. Week-to-week variation among the neighbours is of the same order as the effect — 2025's neighbours run from 206 down to 133 — and in neither year was the holiday week the lowest of the five weeks measured. The clean measurement is the holiday Monday itself, where the drop is −71% to −87%. That is the number to lean on.

### What that is worth, modelled

**This next number is modelled, not measured, and should be read as an order of magnitude rather than a forecast.** Take the 198 new patients the week of Sep 14 needs — the larger of the two weeks, so this is the top end of what one dark Monday is worth — apply Joe's ~80% booked-in-the-prior-seven-days figure, and apply Monday's ~24–28% share of weekday demand on raw totals — the per-day-normalised shares are higher, 25.0% and 29.7%, so 40 sits at the conservative end on that input: **approximately 40 new patients of booking demand** would normally be created on a Monday of that size. The estimate then assumes a closed holiday Monday books nothing at all, and that is the pessimistic end of what Step 5 measures — phones run 71% to 87% dark, but the web form keeps working at roughly −39%, so neither channel actually goes fully dark. That assumption runs the opposite way to the share input: on the loss side 40 is a ceiling, and the net is smaller by whatever share of a normal Monday still books. Applied per appointment date, most of that lands on Tuesday through Friday, September 8–11 — four of the five clinic days inside Labor Day's seven-day reach — where it has to be recovered on top of those days' normal load, against a historical Tuesday-after-holiday catch-up of only +12%. The remainder falls on Monday September 14.

The assumption to name out loud: **Monday's share of total new patients is proxied by Monday's share of web demand signal.** Referral volume and direct phone volume may follow a different weekday curve, and GA4 cannot see either. If referrals are flatter across the week, the exposure is smaller than 40. If referral offices are also closed on Labor Day — likely, but not something this analysis can verify — it may not be.

### The point to land

The four-day week is exposed twice. On capacity: five of seventeen ortho clinic days gone, quantified at 13 patients against the 105 on the tracker in Section A. On demand: its booking window contains a Monday on which phones historically run 71% to 87% dark, and the days that follow do not give it back. The banner week takes about one clinic day of spill and is otherwise filled from normal days.

Both risks are visible **a week before either week starts**. That is precisely what the new one-to-two-weeks-ahead planning cadence exists to catch, and it is the first live test of it. Under the self-report-before-the-fact rule, both go on the table now, not in a retrospective.

## D. What to do about it

The meeting closed the schedule-side options deliberately: no last-minute capacity, no stolen clinic days, and nothing changed in the new schedule's first live week without written approval from both Joe and Katie. Those rules are correct and this analysis does not argue against them. It means the available moves are demand-side and booking-side.

Five recommendations. Each has an owner and a date.

### 1. Front-load booking outreach — and name exactly what moves

**Owner: Joe, with Kristen Jones (PR / physician liaison). Specified and live by Wed Sep 2.**

Be precise about the mechanism, because the seven-day rule decides what each window can actually reach. Outreach run **Wed Sep 2 – Fri Sep 4** converts into appointments roughly Sep 2–11 — this week and the four-day week. Only its long-lead tail, the roughly one in five who book more than seven days out, reaches Sep 14–18. That makes Sep 2–4 the right window for the four-day week, which Section C shows is where the demand hole lands. For the banner week the load-bearing windows are **Tue Sep 8 through Fri Sep 11**, plus referral outreach placed before the holiday for the long-lead share.

Three owned asks, because "front-load outreach" is not something anyone can act on:

- **Joe: name the specific outreach being moved into Sep 2–4** — channel, list size, spend — and confirm it is live by Wed Sep 2. None of that is specified today.
- **Joe, with Kristen Jones: name the referral offices being contacted before the holiday** — how many, and by what date. Referral offices are closed Monday too, so the same front-loading applies; the office count and the date are not yet on paper.
- **Joe to ask Katie by Wed Sep 2 whether Tuesday Sep 8 phone coverage can be staffed above a normal Tuesday.** Katie's call, answer needed by Fri Sep 4. Tuesday is a low-volume call day but not the lowest — on per-day averages Wednesday is lowest at 81.4 and Tuesday second lowest at 84.3, against a Monday of 151.4 — so a Tuesday staffed to its usual pattern is staffed for the wrong day. This is an operations decision and marketing does not make it.

### 2. Run the cancel-and-reschedule recovery queue hard on Tuesday Sep 8

**Owner: Kelley Peters, with Joe. Standing from Tue Sep 8, reviewed daily through Fri Sep 11.**

The kept number arrives around 10:30am the next day. In a normal week that is fast enough. In a week where one of five demand days has already been lost, every cancel that is not refilled the same day is a patient the week cannot get back — the catch-up data says the following days do not absorb it. Capture cancels and reschedules immediately and refill from the queue, every day of Sep 8–11.

This is a demand-side and booking-side move. It requires no schedule change and no approval under the written-approval rule.

### 3. Get the trailing new-patients-per-clinic-day baseline out of the practice system

**Owner: Joe, with Kelley Peters. Delivered into the Friday Sep 4 planning session — the last planning session before the four-day week starts, and so the last point at which this number can still move the Sep 7 target. Wednesday Sep 2 is a count-versus-target check, not a planning session. Standing thereafter.**

The single largest gap in this analysis. GA4 holds no Liine data and cannot produce this number. It has to come from the practice system, by service line, by clinic day, over the recent trailing weeks.

Two specific outputs, both small:

- **Actual new patients per ortho clinic day and per spine clinic day**, trailing. This turns 7.65 and 5.67 from target-derived arithmetic into a measured baseline, and it either supports 92 or moves it.
- **How many ortho clinic days actually exhausted their open new-patient slots.** This is the test that decides whether PA support could raise output at all, or would only add slots to days that already have spare ones.

Kelley's backfill of this week's clinic-day counts, promised right after the meeting, is the other half of this and completes the top row of the Section B table.

### 4. Close the David Mendelson clinic-day discrepancy with Katie

**Owner: Joe, with Katie. This week — before the Friday Sep 4 planning session.**

Gautam believed the new schedule gave every physician three clinic days and says Katie confirmed it. David Mendelson has two, and Kelley's account is that his Monday was always an ASC fill day, never a set clinic day. The 17-clinic-day denominator behind the 130 ortho standard depends on which of those is correct. This is a five-minute reconciliation that protects every forecast built on 17.

### 5. Re-forecast ortho for the week of Sep 7 from 105 to 92 — and do it this week

**Owner: Joe. Recommendation to Katie and Gautam by Wednesday Sep 2, before the week starts.**

This is the recommendation, not a menu.

**Take 92 to Katie and Gautam this week.** The reasoning is Section A: 12 of 17 ortho clinic days is 70.6% of a normal week; 130 at the standard's own productivity on 12 days is 91.8; 105 requires 8.75 new patients per clinic day and an 87.5% fill against a normal 76.5%, which is 14% above normal-week productivity, in a holiday week, in the first live week of a new schedule. Present PA support and a possible softer ASC week as named but unconfirmed upside against 92, with the conditions each would have to meet, rather than as assumptions baked into the number.

**Why pre-declaring beats post-explaining.** The rule set in the meeting is that Joe, Katie and Mitch flag a coming miss before the week starts, not explain it afterwards — Gautam: "If it's going to impact new patient numbers, Joe, you're responsible for the outcome" — including when the cause sits in another team. A 105 that lands at 92 in ten days is a miss that has to be explained, and the explanation will be arithmetic that was fully available on August 31. A 92 declared on September 2 is a forecast that was made honestly against known capacity, and it converts the same ten days from defence into planning. It also gives the four-day week a target that can be beaten, which is worth more to the team than a target that cannot be hit.

Two things this recommendation is not. It is not a request to lower the standard: the ortho standard stays 130 per week on 17 clinic days, and the week of Sep 14 stays at 130. And it is not a request to change the schedule — 92 is what the published schedule produces at normal productivity.

The alternative — carry 105 into the week and explain the gap on September 15 — is the exact behaviour the lessons-learned page was written to end.

## How confident is this

### Measured

- **Clinic-day counts for the weeks of Sep 7 and Sep 14.** Counted physician by physician by Kelley Peters live in the meeting; Gautam restated the week-of-Sep-14 totals (12 spine, 17 ortho) in the same meeting. The week-of-Sep-7 counts have no second source. Self-reported from the scheduling system rather than independently pulled. This week's counts are not yet done.
- **All GA4 figures in Section C and the appendix.** Queried from property 370514163 on 2026-08-31.

### Arithmetic

- 70.6%, 29.4%, 7.65, 5.67, 91.8, 8.75, +14%, 76.5%, 87.5%, 56.7%, and the five-day ortho decomposition. Also the per-day weekday averages and the normalised Monday shares of 25.0% and 29.7%, which are the GA4 totals divided by the number of times each weekday occurs in the 61-day window. These are computations on the two sets of numbers above. They contain no estimate. They can be checked in a minute with a calculator, and they should be.

### Modelled

- **The ~40 new-patient exposure created by the dark Monday.** Built from three inputs: 198 target new patients for the week of Sep 14, Joe's ~80% booked-within-seven-days figure, and Monday's ~24–28% share of weekday web demand. It is an order of magnitude, not a forecast. It depends on the proxy assumption stated in Section C, and where it lands depends on applying the seven-day rule per appointment date rather than per week. It also assumes the holiday Monday books nothing at all — the pessimistic end of Step 5's own measurements, where phones run 71% to 87% dark but the web form only about 39% — so on the loss side 40 is a ceiling, not a central estimate.

### The rate caveat, restated

**7.65 and 5.67 are derived from targets, not from observed output.** They are the productivity the standards themselves imply. The measured trailing equivalent does not exist in this analysis and is recommendation 3.

### What would falsify this

- **Ortho's measured trailing new patients per clinic day comes back materially above 7.65.** Then 105 is closer to reachable than this analysis says, and 92 is too conservative. This is the most likely single thing to move the conclusion, and recommendation 3 is how to find out.
- **Ortho clinic days are shown to be running out of new-patient slots.** Then slot supply is a real constraint, PA support has measurable upside, and the fill-rate framing in Section A understates what is available.
- **Monday's share of total new patients differs materially from its share of web demand.** If referral and direct phone volume are flatter across the week, the ~40 exposure shrinks. If they are more Monday-weighted, it grows.
- **The practice system shows bookings recover after a holiday even though click-to-call does not.** The catch-up test is measured on demand signal, not on booked patients. Booked-patient data would settle it and GA4 cannot supply it.
- **The David Mendelson clinic-day question resolves to three days.** Then the normal-week ortho denominator is not 17 and every rate here shifts.

### Instrumentation caveat

GA4 event history is uneven and this constrains what can be compared. `click_to_call` is clean back to January 2024 — the only event with enough history for the 2024 and 2025 Labor Day comparisons, which is why those rows are call-only. `new_patient_intent` begins April 2026 at 53 events and stabilises in May 2026. `book_appointment_click` and `appointment_handoff_to_zocdoc` have clean full-month data only from July 2026. **The clean cross-event window is about nine weeks, 2026-07-01 to 2026-08-30 — 61 days, holding 8 Mondays and 8 Tuesdays against 9 each of Wednesday, Thursday and Friday.** Weekday comparisons in Section C are normalised to per-day averages for that reason. There is no thirteen-week booking-intent history in GA4 and this document does not claim one.

And once more, because it governs every number above: **GA4 holds no Liine data.** It measures website demand signal. It does not measure booked new patients and it does not measure kept new patients.
