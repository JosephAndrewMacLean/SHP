## 1. Purpose (one paragraph)

Spine, Diagnostic Imaging, and Chronic Pain Management (internally: Pain Management) are **one
patient journey split across schedules that can't see each other** — and imaging is often the
critical path onto a physician's schedule: surgeons want images before the consult, and a scan
needs an order plus prior authorization before it can even be booked. The two people whose
teams live on either side of that seam are Kelley and Mitch. This session is deliberately just
the three of us: small enough to be completely candid, senior enough to redesign the seam, and
structured so **we build the fix together** — one front door, one shared queue, one standard
for how a caller becomes a scheduled patient. The prize is concrete: the spine plan estimates
qualifying and routing the demand *already calling us* is worth **+6 to +9 new spine patients
per week, with the people we already have.**

## 2. The opportunity (system-first — this is about tools, not people)

- **The demand is already on our phones.** In the 32-call review pack, at least 10 callers
  came to us because a competitor was slower, pricier, or silent. Patients are defecting *to*
  us. What they hit next is a system with no capture step, siloed calendars, and fax routing —
  none of which any rep can fix from their seat.
- **The people are already good.** The warmest, sharpest calls in the pack — the participation-
  list check that beat a competitor's price by half, the scheduler who took the callback duty
  on herself, the next-day consult offer — came from Kelley's and Mitch's teams, improvising
  without tools. The pack's fixes are mostly "make everyone as equipped as our best people
  already are."
- **The drop-off happens at the shared front door** — the intake, routing, and cross-line
  handoff layer that no single team owns, which is exactly why the three of us are meeting.
  (166 canceled/no-show patients later self-rescheduled online and kept — proof patients meet
  us halfway when access is easy.)
- **Pain Management looks like it's declining (292 NPs Jan–Jul) — but part of that is a
  measurement artifact** (spine-adjacent pain patients tagged "Pain" instead of
  "Spine/Conservative" — D7) **and part is access friction at the front door** (P1), not
  demand. Pain is the conservative front door that keeps spine patients in-system; fixing
  intake lifts Pain's real number and the spine number together.
- **Imaging facility payer participation is separate from the practice's — and no process yet
  assigns anyone to verify it at intake** (D4 creates the card and names the owner).
- **The clock:** spine is at ~46–48/week against a 65 floor (Aug 30) and 72 goal (Sept 30),
  and rep training is scheduled before Aug 4. Decisions we shape now get trained once.

## 3. How we run the room (kind by design)

This is a blameless working session in the spirit of clinical debriefs and blameless
postmortems: **we critique the system we inherited, never the people in it.**

**Working agreements (read aloud at the top, ~2 minutes):**
1. Everyone on every team did their best with the tools, time, and information they had that
   day. We're here to fix systems, not grade people.
2. We're all here for the same person — the patient with back pain who called and didn't get
   in. Every fix is for them.
3. We talk about calls and processes, not names. If a finding stings, that's the process
   failing a person — not a person failing us.
4. It's safe to say "that one's on my team's plate." When someone owns something, we thank
   them; we never pile on.
5. Speak from your own seat: "I" and "we," not "they always."
6. What's said here is for learning. What leaves here is action items and owners — never
   stories about individuals.

**Practices we'll actually use:**
- **No surprises, ever.** The call-review pack circulates to **no one beyond the three of us**
  until Kelley and Mitch have corrected it and co-signed v1.1 as co-reviewers. Anna and
  Dr. Oddo each get a private heads-up from Joe on the one or two items touching their teams
  before anything reaches them secondhand.
- **Wins first.** We open the evidence with the strongest calls and name what made them work —
  the room defines "good" from its own best performance before looking at any gap.
- **The Just-Culture question.** For every gap: *"what made that the reasonable thing to do in
  the moment — what tool or information was missing?"* — asked before any fix is proposed.
- **Grammar rule for 90 minutes:** every finding names a process noun, never a person. "The
  transfer step loses callers," not "the reps lose callers." Joe enforces it on himself first.
- **Proposal-based decisions.** Fixes are stated as concrete proposals ("every imaging inquiry
  gets name + number captured — trial for 30 days"), disagreement is explicitly invited, then
  we decide, log owner + date, and commit out loud.
- **Physician-sensitive content stays out of this room.** Anything involving individual
  physicians' routing or performance is closed privately by Katie with the physicians —
  clinical leadership, clinical channel. This meeting only notes the dependency exists.
- **Close with appreciations** — each of us names one specific thing another's team did well
  in the evidence. Five minutes, non-negotiable.

## 7. The imaging fast lane — the centerpiece (Mitch owns and co-authors this)

**The problem in one sentence:** imaging is often the ticket onto a doctor's schedule, but
imaging scheduling lives with a different team, on different systems, behind per-site fax
numbers — so the one-roof promise can't be kept on one call, and orders get lost in routing
before a human ever sees them.

**The design principle (stress-tested against how MRI booking really works):** we do **not**
solve this with live call transfers or by having call-center reps book MRIs. The binding
constraint isn't phone connectivity — it's that a scan needs a signed order, prior auth, a
safety screen, and facility-level payer participation before a slot can honestly be promised.
So the fix is a **shared pending queue with imaging keeping the verification gate:**

> **One shared pending-imaging queue.** Every imaging inquiry or received order — any desk,
> any site — lands in one tracked list with a status ladder: *inquiry captured → order
> received → auth initiated → auth approved → safety-screened → scheduled → completed.*
> Both teams see it. The **consult books immediately**; the imaging entry rides the queue as a
> **pended booking** that **Mitch's team verifies (safety, protocol, facility payer
> participation) and confirms within an agreed SLA.** Nobody's patient falls between the desks,
> because there is no "between" anymore — there's a queue with a name on every row.

**What this replaces:** fax-based routing with no confirmation loop — multiple fax numbers,
site-siloed queues, no way to know an order arrived — becomes capture-and-chase: *we* chase the
order at 48 hours, *we* call the patient, and a lost fax is a visible aging row instead of an
invisible lost patient.

**Segment the demand honestly** (so we're judged against what the lane can actually reach):
1. **External order in hand** → pended booking applies today; ordering practice captured for
   the liaison team; results back to the orderer fast.
2. **Our physician orders at a visit** → the biggest slice. Fix is the *checkout handoff*:
   order entered before the patient leaves, queue row created at checkout — target **"imaging
   queued before the patient exits the building."**
3. **No order yet** ("I think I need an MRI") → route, don't refuse: offer the eval visit that
   generates the order. This converts imaging inquiries into new spine/ortho patients.
4. **True same-call scheduling** is realistic mainly for no-auth payer classes (Traditional
   Medicare, Auto No-Fault PIP; Workers' Comp still needs adjuster approval) and
   order-plus-auth-in-hand callers — **and even then imaging still confirms** (safety screen,
   protocol, facility participation) before the slot locks; the verification gate applies to
   every payer class. The queue handles everyone else honestly.

**What stays with Mitch's team, in writing, regardless of anything else** (per the ACR Manual
on MR Safety and standard radiology practice): MRI safety screening ownership and go/no-go ·
protocoling and exam-to-slot/magnet mapping · contrast decisions · the prior-auth verification
standard · capacity and template governance · training and competency sign-off for anyone who
ever books imaging. Schedulers outside imaging can *collect* a scripted pre-screen and
*initiate* a pended booking; only imaging *confirms*. **This is not a re-org, no re-org is
proposed here, and reporting lines are out of scope for this work — full stop.**

**Supporting moves (small, fast, all Mitch-owned or co-owned):**
- **One capacity snapshot** per site/modality, published twice daily with a timestamp,
  **directional, never bookable** ("imaging typically has openings at [site] in [window]; the
  imaging team confirms your exact time within one business day"). Owned and formatted by
  Mitch — paired demand-vs-capacity, so it reads as his case for resources, not a report card.
- **Named backup for every single-person function** — starting with an imaging-side backup for
  the arthrogram scheduler — before any SLA is published. Baseline actual response times for
  2–3 weeks, run the SLA *internally* for a month, and define a degraded-mode rule for absence
  days so a miss is a managed state, not a broken promise.
- **Facility payer participation card** (with Greg) as a hard stop in every booking flow — if
  the plan isn't green-lit for that facility, the row routes to imaging, full stop. Catches of
  out-of-network bookings get reported as **saves**, credited to imaging.
- **Pilot small:** one site, a 2–3-rep mini-pod on the call-center side, sequenced *after* the
  spine-intake script go-live so nobody absorbs two changes at once. Publish the pilot's error
  and rebooking data before expanding.

**How we'll know it's working (queue metrics, all co-owned):** order-to-scan days (segmented:
order→auth-submitted, auth-submitted→approved, approved→scan) · one-call resolution / tandem
rate (consult confirmed + imaging pended in one contact) · scan-before-consult rate for
surgical-intent visits · auth-related denial/write-off rate (the guardrail proving speed cost
nothing) · slot utilization + same-day-cancel rate per site · transfer service levels.
*(Model comparisons, industry sources — MGMA, ACR, AHRA, RSNA — and the full research live
with Joe; ask and he'll share.)*

## 11. Risks, named honestly

- **This reads as a turf move if we get the framing wrong.** Mitigation is structural: Mitch
  owns and co-authors the queue, the capacity snapshot, and the training/competency sign-off
  for anyone who books imaging; integration makes his numbers better (saves, denials avoided,
  no-shows down), not his team smaller; and no reporting-line proposal exists in writing
  because none is proposed.
- **SLAs published before they're real become broken promises.** Baseline first, internal
  month first, degraded-mode rule always.
- **Two changes at once overload the phone room.** Sequence the imaging pilot after spine-
  script go-live; carve pended-queue calls out of handle-time targets; QA scores queue
  accuracy, not just speed.
- **Interim plumbing becomes permanent by inertia.** Write the queue/visibility requirements
  into the ATTR-C.1 system-consolidation spec now; put Mitch on that selection committee.
- **Clinical items stall.** Every §8B item leaves the room with a named recipient and a date
  Joe commits to; the 2-week check-in reviews routing status first.
