# Lessons Learned: What It Takes to Hit a New-Patient Budget

Joe MacLean · SHP executive team · Tuesday, September 1, 2026 · Internal operational document

## The one thing

**We never miss for one reason. We miss when scheduling, capacity and demand fall out of sync — and execution is what comes out the other side when all three are in sync.**

Execution means doing what we said we would do. It is not a fourth pillar. Gautam was explicit on this point: if scheduling, capacity and demand are in sync week after week, there is nowhere left for a separate execution failure to come from. That is why this page has three pillars and not four.

## The year, honestly

- The year will land at roughly **86–87% of plan**.
- Next year's budget is being built to squeeze out a **12–15%** shortfall.
- My own framing, not something set in the meeting: September through December is where we prove the hygiene works, not where we close the gap.
- Gautam's framing: capacity, demand and scheduling are basic marketing and operations. The CEO conversation we want is doubling spine because we doubled the surgeon bench — not missing on hygiene.

## 1. Scheduling

**Claim: a schedule that is still moving cannot be filled.**

- About **80% of new patients book within seven days** of their appointment date. A week's fill is created in the seven days before it, so a schedule change made inside that window arrives too late to be filled.
- We opened and changed capacity too late to matter. A half-day opened on 12 hours' notice does not fill, and it tells physicians the published schedule is not real.
- The plan moved in Q1. Growth was going to be loaded onto Mayo and Bhullar with the Mendelsons reduced; that was audibled. Yacisen's Port Huron-weighted schedule is not built for growth the way Mayo's is.

## 2. Capacity

**Claim: clinic days first, slots second — and when capacity moves, the target has to be rebuilt from it.**

- **The ortho number on the board for the week of September 7 is about 13 new patients above what 12 clinic days supports.** Why we are down to 12: that week is a four-day week — Labor Day Monday — with one ortho physician out on PTO until about Thursday, September 10, and two more losing their Monday clinic. That is **12** ortho clinic days against **17** in a normal week, **70.6%** of normal capacity. Two of the five lost days are Mondays and the rest is the PTO, subject to Kelley confirming whether the physician on PTO normally has a Monday clinic. Spine is unaffected because Kelley has all four spine physicians at three clinic days inside the Tuesday-to-Friday week. At normal-week productivity, 12 days produce about **92** new patients (130 × 12/17). The **105** on the board is roughly 13 above that, and needs **14%** more productivity than a normal week. Those figures rest on a 17-day ortho normal week, which counts David Mendelson at 2 clinic days; that count is being reconciled with Katie this week. If it comes back as three for every physician, the normal week is 18 and these become **66.7%** of normal capacity, about **87** new patients at normal productivity, a gap of roughly **18**, and **21%** more productivity than a normal week. The finding gets stronger either way; the table below is built on 17.

| Week | Ortho clinic days | Target on board | NP per clinic day required | NP per clinic day a normal week implies | Slot fill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal five-day week | 17 | 130 | 7.65 (130 ÷ 17) | 7.65 | 76.5% |
| Sep 7, at the 105 on the board | 12 | 105 | 8.75 (105 ÷ 12) | 7.65 | 87.5% |
| Sep 7, at pro-rata | 12 | 92 | 7.65 (91.8 ÷ 12) | 7.65 | 76.5% |

Slot fill is the target measured against Gautam's own standard of *at least* 10 open new-patient slots per clinic day — read it as a ceiling, not measured fill. The numerator is a target rather than a delivered count and the denominator is a minimum, so real fill is lower wherever slots run above 10, as Kelley says they do. Every figure in the table is computed from our own numbers.

- Spine is the contrast. Spine holds **12** clinic days in both weeks — capacity is unchanged — and its **68** works out to **5.67** new patients per clinic day, a **56.7%** ceiling against the 10-slot minimum. Spine's 68 is capacity-consistent. Ortho's 105 is not. That is a verdict on spine's capacity, not on spine's demand: the short week's own booking window contains Labor Day, and spine is 68 of that week's 173 targeted new patients, so a share of the booking hole below is spine's. We cannot size that share — the Monday pattern we can measure is website demand, and spine leans on referral more than the website does.
- Clinic days are the unit, and we only started counting them on the tracker this week. Gautam's standard: **three clinic days per physician, at least 10 open new-patient slots on each, spread across three separate days** — "you can't have 28 out of 30 slots on two days." Applied to the four spine physicians we count against targets, that standard is 12 clinic days a week; applied to ortho's six full-time physicians it is 18, and ortho's scheduled normal week is counted at 17 — the difference is David Mendelson at two, because his Monday is an ASC fill day rather than a set clinic day. Kelley counted the clinic days physician by physician for the first time in Monday's huddle, and the ortho gap showed immediately.
- The follow-up surge eats new-patient slots. A spike in new patients returns as a follow-up surge roughly **six weeks** later. Without PA support lined up in advance, those follow-ups take the slots we needed for new patients.
- PTO that is announced months ahead has to be planned around, not discovered. Announced is not the same as planned around.

## 3. Demand

**Claim: demand only counts when it becomes a booked new patient who keeps the visit.**

- We optimised to shallow actions. Clicks and calls are not the scorecard. Website analytics measure **demand signal only** — GA4 holds no booking data and cannot report booked or kept new patients — so a rising click count was never evidence of a rising new-patient count.
- We treated spine like ortho. That is my own mistake: I expected spine to grow the way ortho grew. It will not. Spine needs a different, more B2B, referral-led plan. In the **nine weeks** of clean website data we have (July 1 – August 30, 2026), explicitly ortho-labelled pages produced **2,669** new-patient-intent events against **947** for spine pages — ortho runs about **2.8×** spine in website demand signal — while the weekly targets are only **1.9×** apart (**130** ortho versus **68** spine). Spine's number will not come off the website alone.
- Booked is not kept, and kept is what is on the scorecard. The kept number arrives around **10:30 the next morning**. Cancels and reschedules we do not capture and refill that morning become pace we never get back. This is leakage inside demand — not a separate discipline.

## Watchouts — read across all three pillars

**SCHEDULING**

- **Always plan proactively one to two weeks ahead, never the current week. By Monday morning the current week is gone.**
- **Keep six weeks of usable capacity visible.**
- **Lock the schedule early enough to fill it.**
- **This is not a recovery plan. There is no recovery. This is proactive planning.**

**CAPACITY**

- **Protect new-patient slots from the follow-up surge — a spike in new patients returns as a follow-up surge about six weeks later and eats the open slots.**
- **Line up PA support so PAs absorb the follow-ups and physicians see the new patients.**
- **Do not let too many people be out at once. PTO announced months ahead gets planned around, not discovered.**

**DEMAND**

- **Optimise to high-quality converting actions — booked new patients — not shallow events like clicks and calls.**
- **Never book a new-patient slot with a follow-up.**
- **Manage the funnel through to the kept new patient. Kept is what is on the scorecard.**
- **The kept number arrives around 10:30 the next morning. Capture the cancels and reschedules immediately and refill, or the week falls behind pace with no time left to fix it.**

### The first test

Labor Day, Monday September 7, costs us twice. It takes two of the five ortho clinic days the four-day week is down — two physicians each lose a Monday clinic — and possibly a third; the other two or three are the PTO in pillar 2. Which way that splits depends on whether Jeffrey Mendelson normally has a Monday clinic, and Kelley can confirm it. It also takes out a booking day, and because about 80% of new patients book within seven days of their appointment date, the bookings lost on that Monday reach appointments from September 8 through September 14: Tuesday to Friday of the four-day week, plus Monday the 14th. Four of those five appointment days sit inside the four-day week itself. So the demand hole mostly compounds the capacity hole in the same week. The week of September 14 — 29 clinic days, 198 new patients, 130 ortho and 68 spine — takes about one clinic day of spill and is otherwise filled from normal days.

What a holiday Monday actually does, split by how much evidence we have:

- Across three past holiday Mondays, website click-to-call ran **71–87% below** comparable Mondays (Memorial Day 2026 **−87%**, Labor Day 2025 **−82%**, Labor Day 2024 **−71%**).
- Form intent has only one holiday in our clean history — Memorial Day 2026, at about **−39%**. One data point, not a pattern. Phones go dark; the form keeps working.
- The lost Monday does not come back. The Tuesday after Labor Day 2025 did **41** click-to-calls against a **36.8** average for the other Tuesdays in that window. That Monday had dropped to **9** against a **51.2** average. Roughly a tenth of what the Monday lost came back on the Tuesday.

Modelled, not measured: 198 new patients × the ~**80%** booked in the prior seven days × Monday's **23.2–27.8%** share of weekday website demand ≈ **37 to 44** — call it about **40 new patients** of booking demand that a Monday of that size normally creates. Applied per appointment date, most of that lands on September 8–11 and has to be recovered there, on top of those days' normal load; roughly one clinic day's worth spills onto September 14. The estimate assumes a closed holiday Monday books nothing at all, which is the pessimistic end of what the −39% form-intent reading suggests — neither channel actually goes fully dark. It also proxies Monday's share of total new patients with Monday's share of website demand signal; referral and direct phone volume may follow a different weekday curve. Planning two weeks ahead is what gives us the chance to do it.
