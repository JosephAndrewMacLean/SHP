## 1. The thesis

Spine demand is LOCAL and intent-shaped, and we've been serving it generic. The data says:
- **I1 local queries are our best converters** ("spine doctor near me," "back doctor near me")
  — 12–17% conv at ~$111–153 CPA in paid; organic equivalents are free.
- **Every clinic owns its county** — market location-by-location, not one metro brand.
- **Supply is now real per location** (7/13 redesign): Troy has spine Mon–Thu; spine runs
  Friday networkwide; Livonia is the EMG/injection hub.
- **The location layer converts already** (GBP listings ≈ 375+ converting sessions) with
  near-zero investment.

**The play: for each location, publish exactly what a patient there can get, matched to the
three intents, with the booking path one tap away.** Specificity = intent × city × provider ×
day — nobody else in the market does all four.

## 2. The location-intent grid (the system on one screen)

| Intent | What the patient wants | Where they land | What converts them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I1 SOLVE** ("spine doctor near me," "{city} back pain") | An appointment, close, soon | **Location page** (or hyperlocal satellite → nearest clinic) | Roster-by-day module + "often within the week" (if true) + book-online-first CTA |
| **I2 LEARN** ("why does my leg hurt," "sciatica") | Understanding, then options | **Condition canon** (city-agnostic) | The **"spine care near you" module** on every condition page — the I2→I1 geographic bridge (Troy · SH · Livonia · Southfield · PH chips) |
| **I3 CHOOSE** ("best spine surgeon {metro}," "{surgeon name}") | The right surgeon | Team module / bio | Subspecialty labels + **location chips on surgeon cards** ("Dr. Zamorano — Troy") + consult CTA |

Two structural rules:
1. **Condition pages stay evergreen; geography enters via the near-you module**, never baked
   into condition copy (semantic model discipline — no duplicate "{city} sciatica" condition pages).
2. **Hyperlocal satellites** ({city} spine treatment) exist only for cities clearing the D3
   first-party count gate, canonical self-referential, always routing to the nearest staffed clinic.

## 4. The specificity engine (how intent-matching actually gets sharper)

1. **One data source, three website surfaces:** the schedule dataset feeds location modules,
   provider chips, and the call-center's routing table — a schedule change updates all three
   (omni-channel rule). *(The listings/GBP track consumes the same dataset on its own timeline
   — separate workstream per the §3 separation rule.)*
2. **Query→destination matrix** (maintained with the altLabel map): near-me/city terms →
   location pages; condition terms → canons (+ near-you module); surgeon/brand terms → bios;
   "best/top" comparative terms → team module + matching guide (post-V5). Randall implements
   titles/metas per this matrix — his gap-analysis runway pointed at LOCAL queries first.
3. **Near-you module on condition pages** = the I2→I1 bridge: six condition canons × five
   location chips each, driven by the same dataset.
4. **Paid mirrors organic** (Cardinal): geo campaigns match staffed supply — Troy NEW,
   SH/Livonia message-matched, Southfield minimal + podiatry-led, PH none; kill the audited
   location overlap.
5. **Review localization (Rater8, Lane-D screened):** location pages show that clinic's
   reviews, not brand-wide averages — local proof for local intent.
