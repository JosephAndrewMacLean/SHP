Hi there,

If you’ve been living with bunions, hammertoes, or chronic forefoot pain, you’ve probably had the same thought more than once: “I know I should do something about this — but I just can’t take the time off.” I hear it all the time, and it’s completely understandable. The idea of foot surgery, recovery, and weeks off your feet can feel like too much to take on.

But here’s the thing — the conversation around foot surgery has changed significantly. Minimally invasive surgery (MIS) for forefoot conditions is not your grandmother’s bunion surgery. And if you’ve been holding off, summer is actually one of the smartest times to take action.

“This is not your grandmother’s bunion surgery. Have the procedure now, and most patients are back on their feet — comfortably, in regular shoes — well before the holiday season.”

What I Want You to Know

 

01

 

What is minimally invasive forefoot surgery?

Traditional foot surgery often involved large incisions, lengthy hospital stays, and long, difficult recoveries. MIS forefoot surgery uses tiny incisions — often just a few millimeters — and specialized instruments to correct the underlying bone and tissue problems causing your pain. Because there’s far less disruption to the surrounding soft tissue, patients typically experience less swelling, less post-operative pain, and a significantly faster return to normal activity.

02

 

What conditions can it treat?

MIS forefoot surgery can address a wide range of common problems, including bunions (hallux valgus), hammertoes and claw toes, painful corns and lesions related to bone prominence, and certain metatarsal conditions causing ball-of-foot pain.

Many of my patients have tried orthotics, padding, wider shoes, and injections — and found only temporary relief. When the time is right, surgery can offer a lasting solution.

03

 

Why July and August are a smart window

I know it might seem counterintuitive to think about surgery in the middle of summer. But consider this: if you have the procedure done now, most patients are back on their feet — comfortably, in regular shoes — well before the holiday season. Fall and winter activities, family gatherings, and end-of-year commitments are all much more enjoyable when you’re not managing chronic foot pain.

Many patients also find that summer schedules, with kids home and routines shifted, actually allow for a more flexible recovery window than the rest of the year.

04

 

What to expect from recovery

Every patient is different, and I’ll walk you through exactly what your recovery would look like based on your specific condition and procedure. In general, MIS patients are often bearing weight the same day or within days of surgery, and most return to low-impact activity within a few weeks. We’ll put together a plan that works with your life — not against it.

5 signs to watch for

01 You’ve had persistent forefoot, bunion, or toe pain for 6 months or more.

04 You’ve been told you were “not a candidate” for surgery in the past — MIS changes that for many patients.
 
05 You’re dreading another season of the same pain.
