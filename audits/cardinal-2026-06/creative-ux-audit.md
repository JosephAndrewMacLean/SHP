What Did We Review?

01
User Experience

<!-- Slide 4 -->
Built for patients who already know what's wrong: experience does not have a clear path for people still seeking diagnosis, at the start of a symptom-hunt journey. "What Hurts" navigation + pages open straight into conditions and surgical/non-surgical treatments, with no content for someone earlier in the journey, still trying to understand what might be wrong.
Navigation Overwhelm: mega-nav exposes 300+ links across 8 panels in a single hover state, no visual hierarchy between symptom/treatment/provider content; some navigation mega-menu views are so large you cannot see all items
Content Density on Major Pages: Imaging/MRI pages (priority links in main nav) have almost no content about the service itself (just location links)
"Why Synergy" sits too low on nearly every page: Across website and LPs, the credibility/stats should be significantly higher. Sell the why before showing everything else, especially on long multi-scroll pages.
Legacy Remnants: Legacy brand "Mendelson Orthopaedic" still live across functional infrastructure: bill-pay subdomain (payment.mendelsonortho.com), YouTube handle, LinkedIn handle; brand migration to Synergy is incomplete behind the scenes.
Website Experience

<!-- Slide 5 -->
Breadcrumbs: breadcrumbs in certain main pages are confusing; physical therapy for instance is a breadcrumb under ‘treatments > spine, neck, back,’ scale same rules across all breadcrumbs
Compliance: Accessibility handled via bolted-on overlay widget (AccessiBe-style) rather than native WCAG remediation; common pattern, but increasingly a liability flag given recent ADA overlay lawsuits. Current experience breaks WCAG compliance color contrast in two major color interactions.
Easy Fixes // Current Issues:
Hand & Wrist page has leftover template copy: section headers literally read "Non-Surgical Back & Neck Treatments" / "Surgical Back & Neck Procedures" on a hand & wrist page, likely copy-paste residue from the spine template
Homepage mega-nav has a typo ("Disgnostics") and multiple dead-end links ("View All" pointing to #; tablet view menu dropdowns are all broken)
PT page's main CTA wrongly points to a spine specialist
Find a doctor filters on provider page do not seem to work in desktop
Website Experience

<!-- Slide 6 -->
On-site pages, not true paid LPs: These are live website pages with full nav; visitors can exit off-page via any nav item. Consider dedicated, focused paid-media LPs with stripped nav. Content are long and educational, not conversion focused.
Opportunity for Tailored LP Content: Sterling Heights LP duplicates the general Orthopedic Services page content verbatim (canonical tag points back to the parent page); paid traffic sent to a "localized" LP isn't actually getting localized content beyond a swapped location module.
Missing snapshots & candidacy content: No at-a-glance "what we specialize in here" view and no "you may be a good candidate if…" content; both would tighten paid-search message match and likely improve Quality Score
Mobile: experiencing issues with logo legibility, and legibility of hero; consider using the hamburger icon instead of the word ‘menu’ to recover some space in navigation
Landing Pages

<!-- Slide 7 -->
Content Hierarchy: content on this page may benefit from a reorder project; main reasons ‘why’ are in hero, followed by a location component, CTA component, testimonials, find a doctor, location component 2, and then finally we get to the credibility strip for why Synergy; we recommend testing a reorder to prioritize more brand information higher, and downsize some large components (consider downsizing testimonials)
ZocDoc Link: the current Ortho Services zocdoc links appear to all be broken
Focused Conversions: current pages are webpages on site, and have interlinking (as expected) for major site content; however, this can easily distract from the conversion focus, and also primarily be geared around ‘educating’ vs. conversion.
Example: current hand & wrist page lists ‘reasons patients come to see us,’ and ‘non surgical care, and surgical options’ with only the title of the condition/issue; for additional context, users must click to a separate page about said topic. For a paid media destination, keeping high level information and removing the linking capabilities would maintain focus.
Landing Pages

<!-- Slide 8 -->
Location Pages / Example: Sterling Heights
Generic all-provider roster hero: no Sterling Heights or patient cue; same image would work on any location page. Content also states nothing unique about the location.
Buried Value Proposition/Reason to Believe: "Why Choose Synergy" (stats + Trust block) buried in the bottom third, below testimonials and the doctor grid

02
Messaging & Compliance

<!-- Slide 11 -->
Brand Strategy Already Aligned: if Synergy is the intended lead brand going forward (chosen over Mendelson specifically for brand recognition in new markets), the live Mendelson-branded properties is a more urgent fix
Value Props Already Present: Core value props ("no run-around," "everything under one roof," integrated one-stop-shop) are already present in current homepage copy; good baseline consistency to build from
That said, landing pages do not lead with major value props and miss an opportunity to drive a hard-hitting value proposition home with prospects
Missing Spine Differentiation: Spine-specific differentiators (younger surgeons, innovative tech, minimally invasive outcomes) aren't yet distinctly messaged anywhere reviewed; spine page content mirrors the same generic "fellowship-trained, coordinated care" language used across every other specialty, no unique positioning
Disclaimer vs. Claim Tension: Shoulder arthroplasty page pairs a patient-education disclaimer ("this list is for education only") directly next to an assertive efficacy stat ("Over 92% report significant pain reduction"); that juxtaposition is a common compliance flag since the stat can read as a guarantee despite the disclaimer
Substantiation Risk: Stats are attributed to a non-peer-reviewed, internal database; fine as directional data, but would need real substantiation backup if ever challenged on accuracy

03
Social Assets

<!-- Slide 13 -->
Brand Look & Feel: branding on website feels more modern and stronger in comparison to what we’re seeing within organic social (Instagram, Meta, YT, LI) – I’m seeing inconsistent use of gradients, drop shadows, and visual styles in general; thinnest version of font are incredibly hard to read
Format mismatch: 16:9 videos perform poorly in-feed; social needs 9:16 to fill the screen
Recurring typos: Point to a missing creative QA step
What's working: PT-led, UGC-style storytelling with athletes feels organic and relevant; this is the style to scale in organic social and likely in paid as well
Naming Strategy: consider consolidating organic accounts; neither Mendelson nor Synergy PT OT has a significant following, so combining at this juncture is logical
Organic Social Messaging + Strategy

<!-- Slide 14 -->
Openings too clinical: Episodes open on abstract science visuals (molecules, rendered imaging suites); clinical/referral language, not patient language; reorder to lead with the human and demote the science beat
Dated / AI-uncanny feel: Renders read as over-smooth, ~2018 stock medical motion graphics; same modernization issue as organic
Double, off-brand end card: Two sequential cards, both branded to Mendelson Kornblum pointing at mendelsonortho.com; consolidate to one and rebrand to Synergy
Inconsistent end cards across videos: Different colors and fonts video-to-video; reconcile into one template
Unnatural provider angles: Same side-profile issue; the strongest provider content is natural, straight-on office shots (e.g., Dr. Kyle in-office); the model to follow
What's working: The provider + body-rendering layering is effective; natural UGC-style PT storytelling is the strongest asset type
Video Strategy

<!-- Slide 15 -->
Younger and more commercial, driven by service-line shift: Median age 60→56, 18-34 share 15%→21%, commercial payer 42%→52%; growth in Foot (+114%) and Spine (+89%), decline in older/Medicare Pain. Modernize creative, weight spend to paid social/search
A location-specific business, not a metro brand: Each clinic owns its county (61–73%); five distinct personas by age, service line, and payer. Location-curated creative is mandatory
A quantified runway with an address: ~4,800 obtainable ortho Commercial+Medicare patients/yr going to competitors, concentrated in under-penetrated Oakland. Deepen Southfield podiatry now; Troy unlocks Oakland ortho/spine

Guiding Principles
Value propositions lead
Explain the why and show the proof in data — before anything else on the page.
Specificity matters
Every location has different service-line priorities and demographics; paid creative should reflect this.
Intent match drives performance
Landing pages must match campaign intent — if a campaign names a condition or treatment, make it clear and prominent on the page.
One brand
Consistent branding builds trust. Reset the core look and feel across the board, then hold tightly to the standard.
Four durable standards that should guide every decision going forward
