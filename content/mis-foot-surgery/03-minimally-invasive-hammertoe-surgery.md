## H1: Minimally Invasive Hammertoe Surgery

*(Answer-first intro)*

A hammertoe is a toe that stays bent at the middle joint instead of lying flat.
Minimally invasive hammertoe surgery straightens the toe through one or more very
small openings, using fine instruments under live X-ray guidance — without the
longer incision of traditional surgery. For the right patient, that can mean less
tissue trauma and a simpler early recovery. [DR. LEFF: confirm phrasing.]

## H2: Why a bent toe won't fix itself

Hammertoes happen when the muscles and tendons that balance the toe pull unevenly
— from footwear, foot structure, or arthritis. Early on, the toe still flexes, and
pads, roomier shoes, or orthotics can manage it. Over time the joint can stiffen
into a fixed bend that rubs, forms corns, and hurts in every shoe. A stiff,
painful hammertoe generally needs surgery to straighten. [DR. LEFF: confirm.]

## H2: How the minimally invasive version works

- Through small openings, the surgeon releases the tight soft tissue and reshapes
  a small amount of bone so the toe can lie flat. [DR. LEFF: confirm technique.]
- Depending on the toe, the correction may be held with a small implant, a pin, or
  nothing at all. [VERIFY fixation approach.]
- It's outpatient surgery — home the same day, typically walking in a protective
  shoe soon after. [VERIFY]

## H2: Who it's for

Good candidates typically have a painful, rigid (or worsening) hammertoe that no
longer responds to shoe changes, pads, or orthotics. [DR. LEFF: confirm.] Severe
deformity, active infection, poor circulation, or significant arthritis may call
for a different approach — and we'll tell you plainly if so.

Bunions and hammertoes often show up together. If you have both, one surgical plan

## H2: FAQ

**Is hammertoe surgery painful?**
You'll be numbed or sedated during the procedure, and most pain is managed with
elevation, ice, and short-term medication in the first days. Everyone's pain
experience differs. [DR. LEFF: confirm.]

**Will insurance cover it?**
Generally yes when medically necessary — a painful toe that fails non-surgical
care. We verify your benefits before scheduling.

**Can a hammertoe come back?**
Recurrence is possible, especially if the underlying imbalance or footwear habits
continue. Your surgeon will explain how the correction is protected. [DR. LEFF:
confirm.]

**Can you fix two toes at once?**
Often, yes — that's a surgical-planning decision made at your exam. [VERIFY]
