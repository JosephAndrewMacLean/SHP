## ⛔ PHI rule for this workspace (read first)

marketing workspace, not a clinical system. Michael's candidate list (names + DOBs)
lives in the practice's HIPAA-appropriate channels (Marina / clinical side + Michael's
production records). Here we refer to candidates only as **C1–C6**. Production receives
contact info and case context **only after** a candidate's signed HIPAA authorization +
media release. Anyone with the emailed list: don't forward it, don't paste it into
shared docs or task tools.

What we can say here: six candidates, birth years spanning roughly the late 1950s to the
late 1990s — **half are under 40**, which is exactly the diversity Michael wants vs. the

**Why the younger skew is strategically right, not just visually right:** SHP's patient

going. (Also FTC-smart: a range of ages/outcomes reads as typical, not cherry-picked.)

## The workflow (who does what, in order)

1. **Michael → Marina: clinical pass.** Michael shows Marina the first four testimonials,
   then reviews the six candidates with her. For each, Marina confirms — inside clinical
   systems, not by email to marketing/production:
   condition · procedure · treating physician · timeline (surgery → recovery) ·
   outcome is *typical* (FTC) · no clinical/behavioral red flags · patient likely open to it.
2. **Marketing cross-reference (Joe).** With Marina's condition/procedure list (still no
   sharing beyond need-to-know), Joe maps each candidate to a keyword cluster and target
   page using the worksheet below — this is the "specific details from their case
   studies" cross-reference Michael asked for, done at the condition level.
3. **Consent packet BEFORE contact details flow to production:**
   - **HIPAA authorization** — specific to marketing use, naming channels (website,
     YouTube, social, ads), no expiration surprises, revocable.
   - **Media release** (likeness, voice, name/first-name-only option).
   - **FTC disclosure** — any compensation/gift disclosed; results-typical language.
4. **Pre-interview call** (Michael's team; Joe optional) using the question arc below —
   confirm the story beats and the terms the patient naturally uses.
5. **Schedule the shoot**, matched to condition-relevant location b-roll where possible
   (e.g., a knee-replacement story shot in the PT gym ties round 2 to the Livonia videos).

## The v1 → v2 bar (set on the 8/6 review call)

Round 1 captured great whole stories. Round 2 keeps the whole story but adds three
things, systematically, per video:

1. **The handoff moment.** The beat where one part of Synergy handed the patient to
   another — a back follow-up that became "it's actually your neck," PT → surgeon,
   recovery → pharmacy before the drive home. Ask for it explicitly, shoot around it,
   cut to feature it. This is "under one roof" *shown*, never said as a slogan.
2. **The condition + level, named.** Where it's natural, the patient says it the way
   people search: "my herniated disc at L4-L5." (Language map in `keyword-targets.md`.)
3. **A doctor intro quote.** 1–2 lines from the treating physician naming the
   condition/technique — recorded when crews are on site — matched to the treatment
   landing page the video will live on. Joe brokers the physician asks.

## Patient interview arc (steer, don't script)

The goal is the patient *naming* their condition, procedure, and recovery timeline in
their own words — that's what makes the transcript rank and get cited.

1. **Before:** "Take me back — what was going on? What couldn't you do anymore?"
   *(elicits symptom language: "shooting pain down my leg" = sciatica searches)*
2. **The search:** "How did you decide where to go? What were you afraid of?"
   *(elicits the comparison/decision language searchers use)*
3. **The diagnosis:** "What did they find? What did the doctor call it?"
   *(elicits the condition term — herniated disc, stenosis, carpal tunnel)*
4. **The treatment:** "What procedure did you end up having? What was the day like?"
   *(elicits the procedure term; ASC patients: same-day surgery story)*
5. **The handoff:** "Was there a moment they sent you across the hall — a different
   specialist, physical therapy, the pharmacy? How did that go?"
   *(the round-2 focus: under-one-roof shown in the patient's own words)*
6. **Recovery:** "How long before you were back at work / driving / playing ___?"
   *(elicits recovery-time numbers — the biggest question cluster in search)*
7. **Now:** "What can you do today that you couldn't a year ago?"
8. **Direct address:** "Someone's watching this with the same [condition] — what would
   you tell them?" *(snippet-ready, first-person answer)*

**Younger candidates (C1-type):** lean into "in my 20s/30s," return-to-work,
return-to-sport, "I thought I was too young for this" — the exact frames younger
searchers use and round 1 couldn't cover.

## Compliance checklist (every testimonial, before publish)

- [ ] Signed HIPAA authorization on file (marketing-specific, channels named)
- [ ] Signed media release
- [ ] Compensation/gifts (if any) disclosed on screen per FTC
- [ ] Story is a **typical** result, or an on-screen typicality disclaimer is added
- [ ] No outcome promises or "cured/guaranteed/best" language survives the edit
- [ ] Patient saw and approved their final cut
- [ ] Clinical reviewer (Marina or physician) approved medical accuracy of final cut
- [ ] Takedown path documented (patient can revoke; we honor within an agreed SLA)
- [ ] Published as draft-pending-review → SHP sign-off before public
