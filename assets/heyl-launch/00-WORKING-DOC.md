# 1. The rules — check every piece of work against these

## Credentials

| ✅ Say | ❌ Never say |
|---|---|
| Board certified in Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation | Board certified in Pain Medicine |
| Board eligible in Pain Medicine | Pain Medicine board certified |
| Fellowship trained in Pain Medicine | Double board certified |

He sits the Pain Medicine board exam in **September 2026**. Until Credentialing confirms the result
in writing, "board eligible" is the only accurate wording. Directory sites have their own
certification fields — those must match too.

## Training

His Pain Medicine fellowship was in the **University of Michigan Department of Anesthesiology**. His
residency was in **PM&R**, where he was Chief Resident.

**Never** describe him as an anesthesiologist or as anesthesiology-trained. A fellowship housed in an
anesthesiology department is not an anesthesiology residency.

## Procedures

**Never mention, anywhere:** kyphoplasty / vertebral augmentation · long-term opioid management ·
stem cell therapy

**Hold — not at launch:** PRP (until the cash-pay workflow is approved) · basivertebral nerve
ablation · MILD / lumbar decompression

**Careful:** he performs **spinal cord stimulation** and it belongs on his procedure list, but it is
**not his launch identity**. Never headline it, never call him an SCS specialist.

> ⚠️ **`/treatment/kyphoplasty-vertebroplasty/` is live with 8,377 impressions.** If the CMS
> auto-links providers to their specialty's procedures, he inherits it. Check before publishing —
> section 7 of this document.

## Patients we are not marketing to

Widespread or centralized pain · fibromyalgia · heavy chronic opioid management · cancer-related pain

Not a refusal of care — these need a broader pathway or a review first, not direct procedural
scheduling. Marketing shouldn't attract them and the phones shouldn't book them straight in.

## General claims rules

No outcome promises ("eliminates pain," "guaranteed relief"). No superlatives ("best," "leading,"
"#1"). No statistics without a source we can produce on request — **don't reuse the "92% report
significant pain reduction" line**, its substantiation is already flagged. No patient stories or
images without written HIPAA authorization. Grade 6–8 reading level on anything patient-facing.

**Everything clinical is a draft until Dr. Heyl and clinical review sign off.**
