### Clinical focus
> Function-first interventional pain care across the spine, joints and peripheral nerves.
>
> Dr. Heyl's practice combines rehabilitation training with interventional pain fellowship training.
> His evaluation identifies the specific pain generator and the functional limitation it's causing,
> then treats the target directly. Patients with multiple pain sources are addressed sequentially —
> one defined problem at a time.

### Refer for
> **Peripheral nerve**
> Suspected mononeuropathy · focal nerve pain · post-surgical or post-traumatic nerve pain · CRPS ·
> peripheral nerve block or stimulation candidates
>
> **Electrodiagnostics**
> EMG and nerve conduction studies · distinguishing radiculopathy from peripheral neuropathy ·
> distinguishing pain-limited weakness from true neurogenic weakness
>
> **Spine**
> Persistent axial or radicular pain after conservative care · spinal stenosis and neurogenic
> claudication · sacroiliac joint pain · patients who may benefit from a diagnostic or therapeutic
> injection
>
> **Peripheral joint**
> Joint pain not responding to therapy · arthritis pain in non-surgical or post-arthroplasty
> patients · genicular nerve block candidates

### Please don't send *(these need a different pathway)*
> Widespread or centralized pain · fibromyalgia · patients requiring chronic opioid management ·
> cancer-related pain
>
> *These patients may need review or a broader care pathway rather than direct procedural
> scheduling. We'd rather tell you now than reschedule your patient later.*

### Procedures

## Notes for Kristen and Jasmine

**Priority targets, in his own stated order:**
1. Orthopedic spine, neurosurgery and PM&R — **especially University of Michigan and Ann Arbor**,
   where his own relationships are
2. Primary care
3. Neurology — secondary, and specifically for migraine, occipital and facial nerve blocks

Jasmine was named in his onboarding as the person to help map Ann Arbor relationships.

**Two things to lead with:**

**"He has availability."** That's the single most useful thing a referring office can hear. Everything
else is secondary to a patient who can be seen.

**The "please don't send" list.** It feels counterintuitive to lead with what he won't take — but
naming the exclusions is what makes the inclusions believable. Referring offices have been burned by
practices that accept everything and then reschedule. A clear scope reads as competence.

**Ask for individual names.** Dr. Heyl was asked in onboarding to name specific physicians and
practices to prioritize and hasn't answered yet. Chase it — a warm introduction from him outperforms
a cold visit by a wide margin.
