# Refresh — the legacy chronic-pain post (careful with this one)

**URL:** `https://synergyhealth.org/mendelson-orthopedics-is-a-multi-specialty-practice-that-offers-hope-and-relief-for-chronic-pain-sufferers/`
**Status:** plan — needs a decision from Joe before work starts
**Owner:** Randall · **Target:** Sept 8

---

## Read this before doing anything

This old blog post is **the best-performing pain asset Synergy owns.**

| 90 days ending 2026-07-28 | This post | `/treatment/pain-management/` |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks from Google | **275** | 50 |
| Impressions | 12,705 | 9,856 |
| Click rate | **2.16%** | 0.51% |
| Average position | **5.9** | 18.2 |
| Website visits | **139** | 20 |
| Engagement rate | **88%** | 70% |
| **Appointment actions** | **39** | **4** |

A post with the old company name in the URL generates **ten times the appointment actions** of our
actual pain management service page.

**So: don't delete it. Don't casually redirect it.** The instinct on seeing "mendelson-orthopedics"
in a URL is to clean it up. That instinct would cost us the pain line's best converter.

---

## The tension

- **It works.** Position 5.9, an 88% engagement rate, and it converts.
- **The URL carries the old brand.** Our rebrand is documented as incomplete, and this is one of the
  most visible remnants.
- **The content is old.** It likely names physicians who have left and describes services that have
  changed.

## Recommendation

**Refresh the content, keep the URL.** Take the decision to Joe rather than making it alone.

| Option | Upside | Risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Refresh, keep the URL** | Zero ranking risk. Fix the content, keep the performance. | Old brand name stays in the URL | ✅ **Recommended** |
| Redirect to the service page | Clean URL structure | Real chance of losing a position-5.9 ranking and 39 conversions a quarter. The target page ranks 18.2 — we'd be redirecting a strong page into a weak one | ❌ Not now |
| Delete | — | Loses everything | ❌ No |
| Rewrite at a new URL, redirect old → new | Clean URL, content preserved | Same ranking risk, more work | 🟡 Only if leadership insists the URL must change |

A URL with an old brand name is a cosmetic problem. Losing a page that books patients is a real one.
If leadership does insist, do it in the slowest part of the year and monitor weekly — not six weeks
before a physician launch.

---

## The refresh

**Keep:** the URL, the page structure that's working, the general "help for people in ongoing pain"
angle that's clearly matching what searchers want.

**Update:**
- [ ] Current physician roster, including Dr. Heyl from September
- [ ] Current services — remove anything no longer offered
- [ ] Current locations and phone numbers
- [ ] Any remaining Mendelson/Kornblum references in the **body copy** (the URL stays; the visible
      text shouldn't say it)
- [ ] Reading level down to Grade 6–8
- [ ] Modern internal links: Dr. Heyl's bio, the pain management page, condition pages
- [ ] A clear appointment CTA — it's converting at 39 actions per quarter with whatever it has now;
      a proper CTA should do better
- [ ] Add a "medically reviewed by" line with a current date
- [ ] Check every claim against the guardrails — this predates our current standards, so assume
      there's an unsubstantiated statistic or an outcome promise in there

**Don't change:** the URL, the page title *(it's earning a 2.16% click rate — leave it alone unless
you're confident)*, or the overall topic.

## Monitoring — this is the part people skip

Record the numbers above **before** editing. Then check clicks, position, visits and appointment
actions **weekly for four weeks** afterward.

If clicks or conversions drop, revert. You'll have the before-numbers to prove what happened.

---

We have almost certainly got other legacy posts like this — old URLs quietly outperforming the pages
built to replace them. A sweep of every pre-rebrand blog URL, ranked by clicks and conversions,
would probably find several. That's a separate project, but this post is the reason to suggest it.
