## What we need out of one session

Every image below gets used somewhere specific. If time runs short, shoot in this order — 1 and 2
are non-negotiable, 5 is optional.

### 1. Primary portrait — straight-on, in office *(required)*

- Straight-on, eye-level, looking at camera. Natural expression, not a formal studio pose.
- Real clinical background, softly out of focus. An exam room or clinic hallway, not a logo wall.
- White coat and one without, so we can match whichever reads better next to the other physicians.
- **Shoot loose enough to crop square and 4:5** — directories crop to square, the website uses 4:5.

*Why: our patient-experience audit found the natural, straight-on in-office shots consistently
outperform posed studio portraits. This image will appear on his bio, six directory profiles, two
Google listings, and every piece of referral collateral for years.*

### 2. Environmental / working shots *(required, 3–4 frames)*

- Reviewing imaging on a monitor.
- Talking with someone across a desk or at an exam table — **staff or a colleague standing in, never
  a real patient.**
- Hands-and-equipment detail (ultrasound probe, EMG machine) with no identifiable person in frame.

**Do not photograph:** any real patient, any screen showing real patient information, any chart,
schedule, or whiteboard with names on it. If a screen is in frame, it must be off or showing a
stock image.

### 3. Vertical video frame *(required if he agreed — confirm before the shoot)*

- **9:16 vertical, filmed vertically.** Do not shoot horizontal and crop.
- 90 seconds, one question: *"What does a pain specialist actually do?"*
- Lav mic. Static shot. Clean background.
- **No end card until we supply the current one** — several existing videos still end on the old
  Mendelson brand.

### 4. Group / context frames *(nice to have)*

- Him with the pain-management or spine group, if anyone else is on site.
- One wide room shot with no people, for use as a page background.

### 5. Optional, only if he gave permission

He may have approved using his Marine City roots and his college baseball background. **Check
`01-email-to-heyl.md` for his written answer before shooting anything on this theme.** If yes, one
relaxed non-clinical frame — outdoors or in plain clothes — supports the local-press pitch.
