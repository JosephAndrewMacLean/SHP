## The one rule

**Every new patient starts with an evaluation.**

Dr. Heyl was explicit about this: do not promise an injection, a medication, an MRI, or any other
treatment before he has examined the patient.

This isn't a scheduling preference. His whole approach is identifying the specific pain generator
first and treating that. A patient who arrives expecting the injection they were promised on the
phone, and instead gets an examination, has a bad first visit — and it wasn't the physician's doing.

**What to say when someone asks for a specific treatment:**

> "I can get you in with Dr. Heyl for an evaluation. He'll examine you and go through your history
> and any imaging, then talk with you about what he recommends. He doesn't decide on a treatment
> before seeing a patient — that's how he makes sure it's the right one."

---

## Who to book straight in

Book directly with Dr. Heyl:

- Back or neck pain that's persisted and isn't improving
- Pain radiating down an arm or leg
- Numbness or tingling
- Leg pain when walking that improves when sitting or leaning forward
- Joint pain that hasn't responded to therapy
- Pain after surgery
- Referral from another physician for evaluation or nerve testing
- Anyone referred specifically for EMG or nerve conduction testing

---

## Who to route for review first

Not a refusal — these need a different pathway, and booking them straight in sets everyone up badly.

**Route for review, don't book directly:**

| If the caller describes | What to do |
|---|---|
| Pain all over the body, or pain in many areas at once | Route for review |
| Fibromyalgia diagnosis | Route for review |
| Wanting ongoing pain medication or opioid prescriptions managed | Route for review |
| Cancer-related pain | Route for review |
| Asking specifically for kyphoplasty, stem cell therapy or PRP | Route for review — not available from him |
| Unclear whether they're a fit | Route for review. **When in doubt, route.** |

**How to say it without it landing badly:**

> "Let me have someone look at this and get back to you — I want to make sure we get you with the
> right person rather than booking you somewhere that isn't the best fit. Can I take your number?"

Then follow the review process. **Never** say "he doesn't take patients like you," "you don't
qualify," or "that's not something we do." The patient hears a door closing.

## 🚨 Urgent — stop and escalate

If a caller mentions any of these, this stops being a scheduling call:

- **Loss of bladder or bowel control**
- **Numbness in the groin or inner thighs**
- **Weakness that's getting worse quickly**
- Severe pain after a fall or accident
- Back pain with fever
- Back pain with a history of cancer

**What to do:**

> "That's something that needs to be looked at right away, not at a scheduled appointment. I'd like
> you to go to an emergency room or call your doctor's office now."

Then follow the escalation process. **Do not book a routine appointment and move on.**

## What to tell people about him

Short version, when someone asks who he is:

> "Dr. Heyl is a pain medicine physician. He treats back and neck pain, nerve pain, and joint pain —
> and he does nerve testing. He's trained in rehabilitation medicine as well as pain medicine, so he
> looks at what's causing the pain and what it's stopping you from doing."

**Never say** he's board certified in pain medicine — he's board eligible until his September exam.
Never call him an anesthesiologist. If someone asks about his training in more detail, point them to
his page rather than improvising.

---

## What to capture on the call

- **What they can't do because of the pain.** Not just where it hurts. This is the single most
  useful thing for his evaluation, and it's the thing phone notes usually miss.
- How long it's been going on
- Whether they've had imaging
- What's already been tried
- Whether another physician referred them, and who
- **How they heard about us** — required field, no exceptions

## What to say if he's booked out

New physicians can fill quickly, and a long wait quoted badly loses the patient:

> "His next opening is [date]. If that's too far out, I can look at our other pain physicians —
> Dr. Oddo, Dr. Lee, Dr. Kassa or Dr. Singh — or put you on our list if something opens sooner."

Never leave a caller with only a long wait and no alternative. We have five pain physicians.
